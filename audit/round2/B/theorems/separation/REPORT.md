# PART 1. Single-pull reaction destroys the honest benchmark under Bernoulli feedback

## 1.1 What is being separated

The manuscript's introduction (`sections/Intro.tex`) asserts, without proof:

> When delivered rewards are chosen exactly, one pull reveals an agent's choice, so a mechanism can
> react at once, either by deleting an agent that delivers below the announced price ... or by
> blocking a low bidder that later over-delivers ...; here even an agent exerting full effort may
> produce a low reward, so a single pull certifies nothing.

Part 1 turns the second clause into a theorem. The statement proved is stronger than the assertion
in three ways: it holds for a class of rules rather than for one rule, it holds on every instance
with a Bernoulli gap rather than on one constructed instance, and it covers reaction to a high
reward (the SP+SAMF blocking direction) as well as to a low reward (the pure second-price deletion
direction).

## 1.2 The class

Throughout, the scalar model of `sections/setting.tex` is in force: `K >= 2` agents, horizon `n`,
agent `i` has intrinsic mean `mu_i in [0,1]` and capacity `M_i in [mu_i, 1]`; when selected it picks
a target `m_{i,t} in [0, M_i]` and the platform observes `Y_t ~ Bernoulli(m_{i,t})`, conditionally
independent of the past given the target; honest agents satisfy `m_{h,t} >= mu_h` after every
history; `mu_H^* = max_{h in K_H} mu_h`; the platform's reward is
`P(n;S) = E_S[sum_{t<=n} Y_t] = E_S[sum_{t<=n} m_{I_t,t}]`.

**Definition 1 (exclusion mechanism).** A mechanism maintains an *active set* `A_t subseteq [K]`
with `A_1 = [K]`. Let `H_{t-1}` denote the sigma-field generated by everything realized before
round `t` (all past selections, prices, rewards, exclusion decisions, platform coins and agents'
private coins). In round `t`:

1. *price*: the mechanism computes an announced price `tau_t in [0,1]`, `H_{t-1}`-measurable
   (it may be agent specific, that is a vector; only its value at the selected agent is used below);
2. *allocation*: if `A_t` is nonempty it selects `I_t in A_t` using `H_{t-1}` and fresh platform
   randomness; if `A_t` is empty no agent is selected and the round yields `Y_t := 0`;
3. *delivery*: the selected agent `i = I_t` chooses `m_{i,t} in [0, M_i]`, measurable with respect
   to `H_{t-1} v sigma(I_t, tau_t)` and its private coins, and `Y_t | . ~ Bernoulli(m_{i,t})`;
4. *exclusion*: `A_{t+1} := A_t \ {I_t}` with conditional probability `phi_{I_t}(Y_t, tau_t)` and
   `A_{t+1} := A_t` otherwise, the exclusion coin being independent of everything else given
   `(I_t, Y_t, tau_t)`.

Here `phi_i : {0,1} x [0,1] -> [0,1]` is a fixed measurable kernel per agent. Exclusion is
*irreversible* (an excluded agent is never selected again) and is applied *unconditionally*, at
every pull of every active agent.

**Definition 2 (single-pull-reactive).** A mechanism is *single-pull-reactive* if it is an exclusion
mechanism in the sense of Definition 1. The content of the definition is that the exclusion decision
after a pull is a function of that pull's realized reward, the announced price and an independent
coin only. The allocation rule and the price process are unrestricted and may use the entire
history, including any cumulative statistic.

**Definition 3 (responsiveness).** For `rho in (0,1]`, the mechanism is `rho`-*responsive* on a run
if almost surely, at every round `t` at which a pull occurs,

    max{ phi_{I_t}(0, tau_t) , phi_{I_t}(1, tau_t) } >= rho.

In words: at least one of the two possible outcomes of the pull triggers exclusion with probability
at least `rho`.

**Definition 4 (Bernoulli gap).** Agent `i` has *Bernoulli gap at least* `kappa in (0, 1/2]` if
`min{ mu_i , 1 - M_i } >= kappa`. Equivalently, under every feasible honest behavior its delivered
target lies in `[kappa, 1-kappa]`, so both outcomes `Y = 0` and `Y = 1` have conditional probability
at least `kappa` at every pull.

Three remarks on the scope of the definitions, each load-bearing and revisited in Section 1.7.

- *(a)* Allowing `phi_i` to depend on the agent's index costs nothing in the proof. Allowing it to
  depend in addition on a statistic frozen before the reactive phase begins (for instance the
  agent's Stage-1 bid, which is what SP+SAMF's blocking rule uses) also costs nothing: replace
  `phi_i` by `phi_{i,b}` where `b` is that frozen statistic, and read Definition 3 as a bound on
  `max{phi_{i,b}(0,tau), phi_{i,b}(1,tau)}` for the realized `b`. The proofs use nothing about
  `phi` beyond the value of that maximum at the realized arguments.
- *(b)* "Unconditionally" in Definition 1 is essential. A rule that suspends the kernel on some
  histories (for example one that refuses to exclude the last active agent) is not in the class, and
  Section 1.7 gives an explicit instance on which such a rule escapes the conclusion.
- *(c)* "No reward when the active set is empty" is the natural reading of an elimination mechanism:
  it has nobody left to allocate to. A mechanism that keeps allocating to excluded agents is not
  excluding them.

## 1.3 The core lemma

**Lemma 1 (per-pull exclusion probability).** Let the mechanism be `rho`-responsive and let `h` be
an agent with Bernoulli gap at least `kappa` that is honest (so `m_{h,t} in [mu_h, M_h]` after every
history). Then at every round `t`, almost surely on `{I_t = h}`,

    Pr( h is excluded at round t | H_{t-1}, I_t = h ) >= kappa * rho.

*Proof.* Write `p_0 := phi_h(0, tau_t)` and `p_1 := phi_h(1, tau_t)`; both are `H_{t-1}`-measurable,
since `tau_t` is. Condition further on the realized target `m := m_{h,t}`. Given `(H_{t-1}, I_t = h,
m)`, the reward is `Bernoulli(m)` and the exclusion coin is independent of everything else given
`(Y_t, tau_t)`, so the conditional exclusion probability is exactly `m p_1 + (1-m) p_0`. Honesty and
feasibility give `m in [mu_h, M_h] subseteq [kappa, 1-kappa]`. Both summands are nonnegative, so

    m p_1 + (1-m) p_0 >= m p_1 >= kappa p_1,   and   m p_1 + (1-m) p_0 >= (1-m) p_0 >= kappa p_0,

hence `m p_1 + (1-m) p_0 >= kappa max{p_0, p_1} >= kappa rho`. The bound holds pointwise in `m`, so
it survives averaging over the agent's private randomization. `QED`

This is the entire probabilistic content of Part 1 and it is where the model enters: `Y_t` is a
Bernoulli draw whose mean the agent chooses, so *neither* outcome of a single pull has conditional
probability below `kappa`, and *neither* outcome can therefore serve as a certificate.

**Lemma 2 (geometric tenure).** Under the hypotheses of Lemma 1, for every `n`,

    Pr( T_h(n) >= j ) <= (1 - kappa rho)^{j-1}  for all j >= 1,     E[ T_h(n) ] <= 1/(kappa rho).

*Proof.* Let `sigma_j` be the round of `h`'s `j`th pull within the first `n` rounds, with
`sigma_j = infinity` if there are fewer than `j`, and let `A_j := {T_h(n) >= j} = {sigma_j < infinity}`.
Exclusion is irreversible, so if `h` is excluded at round `sigma_j` it receives no further pull:

    A_{j+1} subseteq A_j cap { h is not excluded at round sigma_j }.

`sigma_j` is a stopping time for `(H_t)`, and on `A_j` Lemma 1 applies at `t = sigma_j`
conditionally on `H_{sigma_j - 1} v sigma(I_{sigma_j} = h)`. Hence

    Pr(A_{j+1}) = E[ 1{A_j} Pr(h not excluded at sigma_j | H_{sigma_j-1}, I_{sigma_j}=h) ]
                <= (1 - kappa rho) Pr(A_j).

With `Pr(A_1) <= 1` this gives `Pr(A_j) <= (1-kappa rho)^{j-1}` by induction, and
`E[T_h(n)] = sum_{j>=1} Pr(A_j) <= 1/(kappa rho)`. `QED`

## 1.4 The separation theorem

**Theorem 1 (single-pull reaction collects `O(1)` reward).** Fix `K >= 2`, `kappa in (0,1/2]`,
`rho in (0,1]`. Let `A` be a single-pull-reactive mechanism (Definition 1) that is `rho`-responsive
(Definition 3). Let the instance satisfy: the honest set `K_H` is nonempty, `mu_H^* > 0`, and every
honest agent has Bernoulli gap at least `kappa`. Let `S` be any feasible behavioral profile in which
every strategic agent delivers `0` at every pull (feasible, since `0 in [0, M_i]`; it is also the
myopically optimal per-pull choice of a strategic agent, whose per-pull utility is `1 + mu_i - m`).
Then for every horizon `n`,

    P(n; S)  <=  (1 - kappa) |K_H| / (kappa rho)  <=  K / (kappa rho),

and consequently

    mu_H^* n - P(n; S)  >=  mu_H^* n - K/(kappa rho)  =  (1 - o(1)) mu_H^* n .

In particular `A` does not satisfy `P(n;S) >= mu_H^* n - o(n)` uniformly over feasible behavioral
profiles.

*Proof.* Decompose the platform's reward by the identity of the selected agent:

    P(n;S) = E[ sum_{t<=n} 1{I_t in K_H} m_{I_t,t} ] + E[ sum_{t<=n} 1{I_t notin K_H} m_{I_t,t} ],

rounds with an empty active set contributing `0`. The second term is `0`, because every strategic
agent delivers `0` under `S`. In the first term every target is at most `max_h M_h <= 1 - kappa`, so

    P(n;S) <= (1-kappa) sum_{h in K_H} E[ T_h(n) ] <= (1-kappa) |K_H| / (kappa rho)

by Lemma 2 applied to each honest agent. `QED`

**Theorem 1' (all agents honest; uniform over every feasible profile).** Suppose in addition that
*every* agent is honest and has Bernoulli gap at least `kappa` (so `K_H = [K]`, `mu_i >= kappa` and
`M_i <= 1-kappa` for all `i`). Then the same mechanism satisfies, *uniformly over every feasible
behavioral profile* `S`,

    P(n;S) <= (1-kappa) K / (kappa rho)   for every n.

*Proof.* Identical, with the second term of the decomposition empty: every agent is honest, so
Lemma 2 applies to each, and each delivers at most `1-kappa`. `QED`

Theorem 1' is the sharper reading of the separation. It says a single-pull-reactive rule can be
defeated *without any strategic behavior at all*: on an instance where every agent is honest,
complies at full capacity and never shirks, the mechanism still collects a total reward bounded by a
constant, because it excludes everybody within `O(1)` pulls each. The failure is a failure of
statistical certification, not of incentives.

**Corollary 1 (what retaining the benchmark forces).** Let `A` be single-pull-reactive and suppose
that on some instance satisfying the hypotheses of Theorem 1' it attains `P(n;S) >= mu_H^* n - r_n`
for every feasible `S`. Then its responsiveness on that instance obeys

    rho <= K / ( kappa ( mu_H^* n - r_n ) ).

If `r_n = o(n)` and `mu_H^*, kappa, K` are fixed then `rho = O(1/n)`: with positive probability the
mechanism must reach a pull at which *neither* outcome of that pull triggers exclusion with
probability more than `O(1/n)`. That is the precise sense in which "a single pull certifies
nothing".

*Proof.* Contrapositive of Theorem 1', solving `mu_H^* n - r_n <= K/(kappa rho)` for `rho`. `QED`

Corollary 1 must be read with its quantifier: `rho` is an essential infimum over the pulls of a run,
so the conclusion is that *some* pull is nearly uninformative for exclusion, not that all are.

**Proposition 1 (tenure ratio; no responsiveness hypothesis).** Fix a single-pull kernel `phi` and a
price `tau`, and write `p_0 = phi(0,tau)`, `p_1 = phi(1,tau)`. For `m in [0,1]` let

    N(m) := 1 / ( m p_1 + (1-m) p_0 )   (equal to +infinity when the denominator is 0)

be the expected number of pulls survived by an agent that delivers the constant mean `m` at price
`tau`. Then for every `m in [0,1]`,

    N(m) <= min{ N(0)/(1-m) ,  N(1)/m } .

In particular, a rule that removes an agent delivering `0` within `B` pulls in expectation removes
an agent delivering the constant mean `m` within `B/(1-m)` pulls in expectation.

*Proof.* `m p_1 + (1-m) p_0 >= (1-m) p_0` gives `N(m) <= N(0)/(1-m)`; symmetrically
`m p_1 + (1-m) p_0 >= m p_1` gives `N(m) <= N(1)/m`. Both are valid with the stated convention when
a denominator vanishes. `QED`

Proposition 1 carries no hypothesis at all and is the cleanest form of the separation: under
Bernoulli feedback, the expected tenure a single-pull rule grants to an agent of mean `m` is within
the factor `1/(1-m)` of the tenure it grants to a total shirker. In the deterministic-delivery model
the corresponding ratio is infinite (a complying agent is never excluded, a shirker is excluded on
its first pull), which is exactly the separation the introduction asserts.

## 1.5 Window rules: reacting to the last `k` pulls

**Definition 5 (`k`-pull-reactive).** Fix `k >= 1`. A `k`-pull-reactive exclusion mechanism is as in
Definition 1 except that step 4 uses, after the agent's `r`th pull, the kernel
`phi_i( (Y^{(r-k+1)},...,Y^{(r)}) , (tau^{(r-k+1)},...,tau^{(r)}) )`, where `Y^{(s)}` and `tau^{(s)}`
are the reward and price at that agent's `s`th pull (for `r < k` any fixed convention is used; the
proof only reads the kernel at pull indices that are multiples of `k`). Exclusion is again
irreversible and applied unconditionally.

**Definition 6 (`(rho,k)`-responsiveness).** There are `y* in {0,1}` and `rho > 0` such that, almost
surely, whenever the kernel is read after a pull index that is a multiple of `k`,

    phi_{I_t}( (y*, y*, ..., y*) , (tau^{(r-k+1)},...,tau^{(r)}) ) >= rho .

That is: a run of `k` identical outcomes triggers exclusion with probability at least `rho`.

**Theorem 2 (window rules).** Let `A` be `k`-pull-reactive and `(rho,k)`-responsive, and let `h` be
honest with Bernoulli gap at least `kappa`. Then

    E[ T_h(n) ] <= k ( 1 + 1/(rho kappa^k) )   for every n,

and consequently, on the instances of Theorem 1 and Theorem 1' respectively,

    P(n;S) <= (1-kappa) K k ( 1 + 1/(rho kappa^k) ) .

*Proof.* Group agent `h`'s pulls into consecutive blocks of `k`: block `j` is its pulls
`(j-1)k+1, ..., jk`. Let `B_j` be the event that `h` receives all `k` pulls of block `j` (so
`B_{j+1} subseteq B_j`) and let `F_j` be the history just before block `j` starts. By the
computation in Lemma 1, at each pull of the block the conditional probability of the outcome `y*`
given everything before that pull is at least `kappa` (it is `m` if `y* = 1` and `1-m` if `y* = 0`,
and `m in [kappa, 1-kappa]`). Iterating the tower property over the `k` pulls of the block,

    Pr( all k outcomes of block j equal y* | F_j ) >= kappa^k   on B_j.

On that event the kernel is read at the block's last pull with argument `(y*,...,y*)`, so exclusion
occurs there with conditional probability at least `rho`. Hence
`Pr(B_{j+1}) <= (1 - rho kappa^k) Pr(B_j)`, so `Pr(B_j) <= (1 - rho kappa^k)^{j-1}` and the expected
number of *completed* blocks is at most `1/(rho kappa^k)`. Agent `h`'s pulls consist of the
completed blocks plus at most `k-1` pulls of one incomplete block, so
`E[T_h(n)] <= k/(rho kappa^k) + k`. The reward bounds then follow exactly as in Theorems 1 and 1'.
`QED`

**Corollary 2 (the window must grow with the horizon).** Suppose a `k`-pull-reactive,
`(rho,k)`-responsive mechanism attains `P(n;S) >= mu_H^* n - r_n` with `r_n = o(n)` on an instance
satisfying the hypotheses of Theorem 1'. Then

    k ( 1 + 1/(rho kappa^k) ) >= ( mu_H^* n - r_n ) / ( (1-kappa) K ),

so for fixed `rho, kappa, K, mu_H^*`,

    k >= ( log n - log k - O(1) ) / log(1/kappa)  =  Omega( log n / log(1/kappa) ).

*Proof.* Rearrange Theorem 2 and take logarithms. `QED`

Corollary 2 is the quantitative form of the separation and is, in my view, the most informative
single sentence Part 1 produces: a rule that certifies on a fixed window must use a window of length
`Omega(log n)`. That is the order of the longest run of adverse draws an honest agent produces in
`n` pulls, and it is why the manuscript's monitor uses a *cumulative* statistic with a boundary
`b_n(r) = sqrt(r log(2 n^4)/2)`, which tolerates deficits on a growing scale rather than a fixed one.

## 1.6 The abstract statement behind both theorems

**Proposition 2 (bounded reaction time under compliance implies linear loss).** Let `A` be any
mechanism with irreversible exclusion and no reward on rounds with an empty active set (Definition 1
steps 2 and 4, with step 4's kernel arbitrary, possibly history dependent). Consider an instance in
which every agent is honest with `M_i <= 1 - kappa` and `mu_H^* > 0`. If, under some feasible
profile `S`, every agent's expected number of pulls before exclusion is at most `B`, then

    P(n;S) <= (1-kappa) K B,   hence   mu_H^* n - P(n;S) >= mu_H^* n - (1-kappa) K B.

*Proof.* `P(n;S) = E[sum_t m_{I_t,t}] <= (1-kappa) sum_i E[T_i(n)] <= (1-kappa) K B`, since
`T_i(n)` is at most the number of pulls `i` receives before exclusion. `QED`

Theorems 1, 1' and 2 are the two instantiations in which the hypothesis `E[T_i(n)] <= B` is
*derived* rather than assumed: `B = 1/(kappa rho)` for single-pull rules and
`B = k(1 + 1/(rho kappa^k))` for `k`-window rules. Proposition 2 by itself is close to a tautology
and is recorded only because it isolates what has to be proved.

## 1.7 What Part 1 does not cover, and why each hypothesis is needed

Each hypothesis of Theorem 1' is matched by an instance or a rule that escapes the conclusion. None
of these escapes is a defect of the proof; they delimit the class.

1. **Bernoulli gap is necessary.** If some honest agent has `M_h = 1` and the rule reacts only to a
   low reward (`phi(1,tau) = 0`), then that agent delivering `m = 1` produces `Y = 1` at every pull,
   is never excluded, and the mechanism collects `n`. So `kappa > 0` cannot be dropped. The mirror
   example with `mu_h = 0` and a rule reacting only to a high reward shows the other half.
2. **Responsiveness is necessary.** With `rho = 0` (`phi` identically zero) the mechanism never
   excludes and is an ordinary bandit algorithm; the manuscript's own `thm:sharp-robustness` then
   gives the honest benchmark. The theorem therefore says nothing about non-reactive rules, and in
   particular it is *not* a lower bound for the problem: `MESP` attains
   `mu_H^* n - O(n^{2/3}(log n)^{1/3})` under arbitrary behavioral play.
3. **Unconditional application and irreversibility are necessary.** Two explicit escapes:
   - *Reactivation.* `MESP` excludes nobody permanently: a triggered switch restarts EXP3 over all
     `K` agents with fresh weights, restoring every honest agent as a comparator
     (`sections/Private_information_setting.tex`, Stage 3). Definition 1 excludes `MESP` for this
     reason alone, before any question about which statistic is used.
   - *Protecting the last active agent.* Take `K = 2` with one honest agent (`mu = M = 1/2`) and one
     strategic agent delivering `0`. A rule that applies `phi(y,tau) = 1{y < tau}` at every pull
     *except* that it never excludes the last active agent will, once the shirker is excluded, keep
     the honest agent forever and collect `n/2 - O(1)`. This rule is not in the class, because its
     exclusion decision depends on `|A_t|`, a cumulative statistic of the whole history. I did not
     find a repair covering such rules and did not assert one: see Section 1.9.
4. **Cumulative monitors are outside the class by construction.** `MESP`'s test
   `Z_r < r tau - b_n(r)` reads the running sum `Z_r` of the winner's primary rewards, not the
   single reward `Y_r`. So does the running-average test of Braverman et al.'s Mechanism 2
   (Section 1.8.4).
5. **What the theorem does not say.** It bounds `P(n;S)` only. It says nothing about revenue against
   `M_(2) n`, about equilibrium, about monotonicity or fairness, about the optimal rate for the
   problem, or about mechanisms that use a single pull for something other than exclusion (for
   instance to update a price). It gives no lower bound on the `n^{2/3}` rate of `thm:private-mesp`;
   that is recorded as open in `MATH_CANDIDATE_LEDGER.md` Section 6.6 and Part 1 does not touch it.
6. **Finite-`n` reading.** The conclusion is a constant bound `K/(kappa rho)` on the total reward
   *for every* `n`, so no asymptotic regime is hidden: the loss is linear from the first horizon at
   which `mu_H^* n` exceeds that constant.
7. **The honest agents' own behavior.** The bound holds for *every* honest behavior, including full
   capacity delivery; honest agents are not assumed to be adversarial. Only the strategic agents'
   behavior is chosen adversarially in Theorem 1, and Theorem 1' needs no strategic agent at all.

## 1.8 Where the two prior mechanisms sit relative to the class

Written from the primary sources of Section 0, not from the manuscript. The manuscript's own
description of these mechanisms was not used as evidence.

### 1.8.1 The two prior models

*Esmaeili, Shin, Slivkins (arXiv:2312.07929v2), Section 3.1, "Reward Modification".* Transcribed:
at round `t` the algorithm pulls arm `I_t` and a reward `r_{I_t}(t) in [0,1]` is sampled from the
static distribution `D_{I_t}` whose mean is `mu_{I_t}`; **after seeing the reward**, arm `I_t` can
modify it by adding a value of effort `c_{I_t}(t)`, leading to the final reward
`r~_{I_t}(t) = r_{I_t}(t) + c_{I_t}(t)`, and the algorithm observes only `r~_{I_t}(t)`. Agents can
lift rewards up to a maximum value `M_i`. So delivery is *exact*: an agent that wants to hand over a
specific number `v <= M_i` can do so with probability one, by choosing `c = v - r`. This confirms
the manuscript's description of the model ("the selected agent observes its realized reward and
edits it before the platform sees it, so delivered performance is effectively chosen").

*Braverman, Mao, Schneider, Weinberg (PMLR v99), Section 2.2.* Each round the pulled arm receives a
private reward `v_{i,t}` drawn from `D_i` and passes along an amount `w_{i,t}` to the principal,
keeping `v_{i,t} - w_{i,t}`. In the **unrestricted payment model**, which is the model of all their
positive results, `w_{i,t}` may be *any* value in `[0,1]`, independent of `v_{i,t}`. Delivery is
exact here too. Non-strategic arms are those with `w_{i,t} = v_{i,t}`, whose deliveries are
therefore stochastic; this is their analogue of an honest agent.

### 1.8.2 PURE-SP (Esmaeili et al., Algorithm 2): **in the class**

Algorithm 2, line 5, pulls the arms of `A_{m'}` (those that bid the highest value) in a cycling
manner, with line 5A: *delete arm `i` if it gives a value less than `m'` and update `A_{m'}`*. This
is exactly Definition 1 with

- announced price `tau_t = m'` (the second highest bid, announced to all arms in line 3),
- kernel `phi(y, tau) = 1{ y < tau }`, applied at every pull of every active arm,
- irreversible exclusion (line 5A removes the arm from `A_{m'}`, and line 5 only pulls arms of
  `A_{m'}`), and no reward once `A_{m'}` is empty.

It is `rho`-responsive with `rho = 1` on every history with `m' > 0`, since `phi(0, m') = 1`. So
**PURE-SP is in the class of Theorem 1**, and Theorem 1' applies to it verbatim once its
deterministic delivery is replaced by Bernoulli delivery.

Important honest qualification. Esmaeili et al. already prove a failure statement for PURE-SP in
*their own* model. Their Theorem 7.1 reads: *for PURE-SP (Algorithm 2) there exists an instance
where even if the bidding is done truthfully and all of the agents are honest, if an agent at a
subsequent pull deviates from the equilibrium strategy with probability `epsilon > 0` then with
probability at least `1 - 1/n` the revenue `P(n) = o(n)`*; the proof (their Claim B.28) is the
geometric computation `1 - (1-epsilon)^tau >= 1 - n^{-2}` at
`tau = ceil( 2 ln n / ln(1/(1-epsilon)) )`, giving `P(n) <= K tau + 2K = O(K log n)`. Relative to
that statement:

- Their failure needs an *exogenous trembling hypothesis*. In their model the agents can deliver
  exactly `M_top`, hence survive forever, so an independent deviation probability `epsilon > 0` must
  be assumed. Their bound degrades as `epsilon -> 0` and is vacuous at `epsilon = 0`.
- Theorem 1' needs no such hypothesis. In the Bernoulli model the tremble is *forced by the model*
  at rate `kappa = min{mu_h, 1 - M_h}`, a property of the instance and not an assumption about
  behavior. No behavior, honest or strategic, avoids it.
- Theorems 1 and 1' also cover a class rather than one algorithm, cover reaction to a high reward as
  well as to a low one, and hold on every instance with a Bernoulli gap rather than on one
  constructed instance.

The correct claim for the paper is therefore: *the phenomenon is recognized in the deterministic
model as a failure under trembling hands (Esmaeili et al., Theorem 7.1); under Bernoulli delivery it
becomes unconditional and applies to every single-pull-reactive rule.* The paper must not claim the
observation itself is new.

### 1.8.3 SP+SAMF (Esmaeili et al., Algorithm 1): **the blocking rule is in the class, the mechanism is not**

Algorithm 1, line 4A: *if an arm `i` has `m_i <= m'`, but gives reward `r > m'`, then block the arm
(never play it again)*. As an exclusion rule this is Definition 1 with kernel
`phi_{i,b}(y, tau) = 1{ b <= tau } 1{ y > tau }`, where `b = m_i` is the arm's bid, a statistic
frozen at the end of the bidding phase (Remark (a) of Section 1.2 covers this). Blocking is
irreversible ("never play it again"). It is responsive in the *high reward* direction: on histories
with `m_i <= m'` and `m' < 1` it has `phi(1, m') = 1`, so `rho = 1`.

Consequence under Bernoulli delivery: Theorem 1 applies to an honest low bidder. An honest agent
with `mu_h > 0` that bid at or below the price produces `Y = 1` with probability at least `mu_h` at
every pull, so it is blocked within `1/mu_h` pulls in expectation although its *mean* never exceeded
the price. This is the mirror image of the PURE-SP pathology and is why Definition 3 is stated with
`max{phi(0,tau), phi(1,tau)}` rather than with `phi(0,tau)` alone.

The mechanism as a whole is *not* in the class: SP+SAMF's phase (B) runs a SAMF bandit algorithm
(UCB or epsilon-greedy) over all arms and line 4A is a blocking rule layered on top. Theorem 1
therefore applies to SP+SAMF's *blocking rule*, and its conclusion is that under Bernoulli delivery
that rule would block every honest agent whose bid is at or below the price within `O(1)` pulls in
expectation. It does not say that SP+SAMF as published fails, since SP+SAMF lives in the
deterministic-delivery model, where a complying arm never exceeds `m'`.

### 1.8.4 Braverman et al., Mechanism 1: **in the class**; Mechanism 2: **not in the class**

*Mechanism 1 (Section 4.1), line 3.* Transcribed: *tell arm `i*` the value of `w0`; play arm `i*`
for `R = T - (u+2)K - 1` rounds; **if arm `i*` ever reports a value different from `w0`, stop
playing it immediately**; if arm `i*` always gives `w0`, play it for one bonus round*. This is
Definition 1 with price `tau = w0` and kernel `phi(x, tau) = 1{ x != tau }`, applied at every pull,
irreversibly. **Mechanism 1 is in the class.** It is safe in their model only because the
unrestricted payment model lets the arm hand over exactly `w0` with probability one. Under Bernoulli
delivery any agent, complying or not, fails the test with probability at least `kappa` per pull, and
Theorem 1' applies.

*Mechanism 2 (Section 4.2), line 3.* Transcribed: *tell arm `i*` the value of `w0`; play arm `i*`
for `R = T - (u+3)BK` rounds; **if arm `i*` ever reports values with average less than `w0 - M` in
any round after `B` rounds in this step, stop playing it immediately**; if arm `i*` gives average no
less than `w0 - M`, play it for `B` bonus rounds*. Here `B = 2^{1/4} T^{3/4}/mu_min` and
`M = 8 B^{-1/2} ln(KT)`. This test reads a *running average*, that is a cumulative statistic, with a
concentration buffer `M` and a burn-in of `B` pulls. **Mechanism 2 is not in the class of
Definition 1**, and Theorem 1 says nothing about it.

This is the finding in Part 1 that most affects how the paper should position itself, so I state it
plainly. Braverman et al. introduce the buffered running-average test explicitly in order to
tolerate stochastic deliveries by non-strategic arms: their text says that adding "a tiny buffer
between the current reported average and `w0`" gives the mechanism "the added property that it works
even when there are a mixture of strategic and non-strategic arms", and their abstract states that
the mechanism "maintains its guarantee if the arms are non-strategic (`x_a = v_a`), and also if
there is a mix of strategic and non-strategic arms". Structurally that is the same idea as `MESP`'s
monitor. The documented differences:

| | Braverman et al. Mechanism 2 | `MESP` (manuscript) |
|:-|:-|:-|
| statistic | running average of the winner's reports | running sum `Z_r` of the winner's primary rewards |
| boundary | linear: average `< w0 - M`, fixed `M = 8 B^{-1/2} ln(KT)`, tested only after `B` pulls | `Z_r < r tau - b_n(r)`, `b_n(r) = sqrt(r log(2n^4)/2)`, tested from the first pull, time uniform |
| who is stochastic | only non-strategic arms; a strategic arm delivers exactly | every agent, including a fully complying one |
| after a trigger | stop playing that arm; the mechanism proceeds to its later phases | restart EXP3 over all `K` agents with fresh weights, restoring every agent |
| guarantee under arbitrary behavior | revenue `mu^0 T - o(T)` at any `o(T)`-Nash equilibrium | honest benchmark `mu_H^* n - O(n^{2/3}(log n)^{1/3})` for *every* feasible behavioral profile, not only at equilibrium |

My reading, stated as a judgement and not as a proved claim: the manuscript's novelty relative to
Braverman et al. is the *anytime* boundary (no burn-in, valid from the first pull, scale `sqrt(r)`
rather than a fixed margin) and the *restart* fallback that makes the honest guarantee hold off
equilibrium. It is not the idea of monitoring a cumulative statistic against the price with a
concentration margin. The manuscript should say so; a claim that the paper is the first to replace a
single-pull certificate by a cumulative one would be wrong.

## 1.9 Least secure step in Part 1

The mathematics of Lemmas 1 and 2 and of Theorems 1, 1' and 2 is elementary and I consider it
secure; the numerical check of Section 3.1 agrees with it to three digits.

The step I consider least secure is **the definition of the class, not any proof inside it**, and
specifically the clause "unconditionally, at every pull of every active agent" in Definition 1
step 4. It is what rules out the last-active-agent escape of Section 1.7 item 3, and a referee can
reasonably object that a designer would never apply an exclusion rule to the last survivor. I looked
for a strengthening covering rules with at most `K-1` exclusions and did not find a proof. The
natural attempt (a bait-and-switch instance on which a rule forced to commit to one agent forever
loses a linear amount) breaks down because such a rule is not forced to *exclude* the agents it
stops pulling, so its allocation may remain adaptive and no commitment is forced. I record this as
open rather than assert it.

The second least secure point is the measurability bookkeeping in Lemma 2: I use conditioning at the
stopping times `sigma_j` and assert `A_{j+1} subseteq A_j cap {not excluded at sigma_j}`. This is
standard, but it is the only place where a careless formalization of "the agent receives its `j`th
pull" could hide an error, so a second prover should re-read it.

# PART 2. Grid-EXP3: the honest benchmark against arbitrary behavior and arbitrary contexts

This is the result recorded as "PROVED IN OUTLINE" in `audit/round2/B/MATH_CANDIDATE_LEDGER.md`
Section 6.3. Part 2 writes it out completely. It is independent of Part 1.

Why it matters: every contextual guarantee currently in the manuscript restricts deviations to the
class `S_HDS` of maps fixed before play (contract Section 12) and assumes i.i.d. context arrivals
(contract Section 11). Those are the two most obvious targets for a referee. Theorem 3 below gives
one contextual statement that holds against arbitrary nonanticipating behavior and an arbitrary
oblivious context sequence, at the same exponent as the manuscript's `thm:grid-ucb-robust`.

**It is a robustness statement and nothing else. It gives no monotonicity, no fairness among top
agents, no sharp adaptivity, no equilibrium, and no revenue guarantee against `M_star`. This is
stated again in Section 2.7 and must appear wherever the result is used.**

## 2.1 The mechanism: Grid-EXP3

Input: horizon `n`, number of agents `K >= 2`, ambient dimension `D = K d`.

- Grid: `b_n := ceil( n^{1/(D+2)} )`, `h_n := 1/b_n`, and the `J_n := b_n^D` half-open cubes of side
  `h_n` that tile `[0,1]^D` (closed on the far face along each axis). Write `C(X)` for the cell of
  `X`. The exact position of `X` inside its cell is used only to locate the cell (the rule is
  *cell blind*).
- State per cell `C`: a visit counter `V_C` (initially `0`) and a weight vector
  `w_C in R_{>0}^K`.
- Epoch schedule per cell, in *local* time: epoch `k >= 0` covers the cell's local visits
  `2^k, 2^k + 1, ..., 2^{k+1} - 1`, hence has planned length `2^k`.
- Round `t`: observe `X_t`, let `C := C(X_t)` and increment `V_C`; write `s := V_C`.
  If `s = 2^k` for some integer `k >= 0`, start epoch `k`: set `w_C := (1,...,1)` (fresh weights) and
  ```
  gamma_k := min{ 1/2 , sqrt( K log K / 2^k ) },     eta_k := gamma_k / K .
  ```
  Then with `q := w_C / sum_j w_{C,j}` and `p := (1 - gamma_k) q + gamma_k/K`, draw `I_t ~ p` with
  fresh randomness, observe `Y_t`, and update
  ```
  hatY_i := Y_t 1{I_t = i} / p_i ,     w_{C,i} := w_{C,i} exp( eta_k hatY_i )   for all i.
  ```

The mechanism needs `n`, `K` and `D`. It needs neither `L`, nor the context law, nor the identity of
the honest agents, nor any capacity. The per-cell epoch schedule replaces the manuscript's
known-cell-horizon assumption; no cell horizon is ever needed.

## 2.2 Hypotheses

- **(H1) Oblivious exogenous contexts.** The context sequence `x_1, ..., x_n in [0,1]^D` is fixed
  before play. It may be chosen adversarially with knowledge of the mechanism's description, but not
  of its realized randomness. *Random* exogenous contexts are covered whenever the context process
  is independent of the platform's randomization and of the reward seeds: condition on the context
  sequence and integrate (Step 1 of the proof).
- **(H2) Arbitrary nonanticipating behavior.** Let `H_{t-1}` be a sigma-field containing: the entire
  context sequence `x_1,...,x_n`, all selections, targets, rewards and platform coins of rounds
  `1,...,t-1`, and all agents' private coins; and *not* containing the platform's round-`t`
  selection coin or the round-`t` Bernoulli seed. The platform draws `I_t` from an
  `H_{t-1}`-measurable distribution `p_t` using randomness independent of `H_{t-1}` and of the
  round-`t` seed. The selected agent then chooses `m_{I_t,t} in [0, M_{I_t}(x_{I_t,t})]`, measurable
  with respect to `H_{t-1} v sigma(I_t)`, and `Y_t | (H_{t-1}, I_t, m) ~ Bernoulli(m)`. Honest agents
  satisfy `m_{h,t} >= mu_h(x_{h,t})` after every history. Agents may know the entire context sequence
  in advance, may condition on their whole cross-cell history, may randomize, and may change their
  behavior arbitrarily across phases, cells and time. This is strictly larger than the contract's
  `B^ctx_{i,n}` (contract Section 5) and strictly larger than `S_HDS`.
- **(H3) Regularity.** Each intrinsic function `mu_i : [0,1]^d -> [0,1]` is `L`-Lipschitz in the
  Euclidean norm. Capacities need no regularity whatsoever.
- **(H4)** The honest set `K_H` is nonempty.

Write `b(X) := max_{h in K_H} mu_h(x_h)` as in the contract, and `P(n) := E[ sum_{t<=n} Y_t ]`.

## 2.3 Lemma A: the per-epoch selected-reactive EXP3 lemma

This is the manuscript's `lem:selected-reactive-exp3` with two changes, both of which are needed and
neither of which is in the manuscript: the realized number of rounds may be *shorter* than the
length used to tune `gamma`, and the filtration may carry the entire context sequence. As instructed,
it is proved here from the potential argument directly, not by citing EXP3.1 (Auer, Cesa-Bianchi,
Freund, Schapire 2002), whose doubling trick is on the estimated cumulative gain rather than on
elapsed time and therefore does not give what is needed here.

**Lemma A.** Fix `K >= 2`, an integer `H_0 >= 1`, `gamma := min{1/2, sqrt(K log K / H_0)}`,
`eta := gamma/K`, and initial weights `w_{i,1} = 1`. Let `(G_s)_{s >= 0}` be a filtration and let
`T <= H_0` be a *deterministic* number of rounds. Suppose that for `s = 1, ..., T`:

- `p_s` is `G_{s-1}`-measurable, given by the displayed EXP3 formulas from the weights, so
  `p_{i,s} >= gamma/K`;
- `I_s` is drawn from `p_s` with randomness independent of `G_{s-1}` and of the round's reward
  randomness;
- `Y_s in [0,1]` and `r_{i,s} := E[ Y_s | G_{s-1}, I_s = i ]`;
- a fixed comparator `j` satisfies `r_{j,s} >= v` for all `s <= T`.

Then
```
E[ sum_{s=1}^{T} Y_s ]  >=  v T  -  R_K(H_0),        R_K(H) := 3 sqrt(K H log K) + 2 K log K.
```

*Proof.* Write `W_s := sum_i w_{i,s}`, so `W_1 = K`. Since `p_{i,s} >= gamma/K = eta` and
`Y_s in [0,1]`, we have `0 <= eta hatY_{i,s} = eta Y_s 1{I_s=i}/p_{i,s} <= eta/p_{i,s} <= 1`. Using
`e^x <= 1 + x + x^2` on `[0,1]` and `q_{i,s} = w_{i,s}/W_s`,
```
W_{s+1}/W_s = sum_i q_{i,s} exp(eta hatY_{i,s})
            <= 1 + eta sum_i q_{i,s} hatY_{i,s} + eta^2 sum_i q_{i,s} hatY_{i,s}^2 .
```
Taking logarithms, using `log(1+u) <= u`, and telescoping over `s = 1,...,T`,
```
log W_{T+1} - log K  <=  eta sum_{s<=T} sum_i q_{i,s} hatY_{i,s}
                         + eta^2 sum_{s<=T} sum_i q_{i,s} hatY_{i,s}^2 .
```
Also `log W_{T+1} >= log w_{j,T+1} = eta sum_{s<=T} hatY_{j,s}`. Dividing by `eta`,
```
sum_{s<=T} hatY_{j,s}  <=  (log K)/eta + sum_{s<=T} sum_i q_{i,s} hatY_{i,s}
                           + eta sum_{s<=T} sum_i q_{i,s} hatY_{i,s}^2 .     (*)
```
Inequality `(*)` is pathwise and holds for every prefix length `T`; this is the only place where the
prefix version differs from the manuscript's statement, and no property of `T` beyond
`T <= H_0` is used.

Now take conditional expectations. Given `G_{s-1}`, the index `I_s` has law `p_s` and is independent
of the round's reward randomness, so
```
E[ hatY_{i,s} | G_{s-1} ] = p_{i,s} r_{i,s} / p_{i,s} = r_{i,s} ,
```
and, since `Y_s^2 <= Y_s` for `Y_s in [0,1]` and `q_{i,s} <= p_{i,s}/(1-gamma)`,
```
E[ sum_i q_{i,s} hatY_{i,s}^2 | G_{s-1} ] <= sum_i q_{i,s} r_{i,s}/p_{i,s}
                                          <= (1-gamma)^{-1} sum_i r_{i,s} <= K/(1-gamma) .
```
Put `Q_s := sum_i q_{i,s} r_{i,s}` and note `E[Y_s | G_{s-1}] = sum_i p_{i,s} r_{i,s} >= (1-gamma) Q_s`.
Taking total expectations in `(*)` and using `r_{j,s} >= v` and `eta = gamma/K`,
```
v T  <=  (K log K)/gamma + sum_{s<=T} E[Q_s] + (gamma/K) T K/(1-gamma)
     <=  (K log K)/gamma + (1-gamma)^{-1} E[ sum_{s<=T} Y_s ] + gamma T/(1-gamma) .
```
Multiplying by `(1-gamma) <= 1` and rearranging, and using `v <= 1`,
```
E[ sum_{s<=T} Y_s ]  >=  (1-gamma) v T - gamma T - (K log K)/gamma
                     >=  v T - 2 gamma T - (K log K)/gamma .
```
It remains to bound the loss `2 gamma T + (K log K)/gamma` by `R_K(H_0)`, using `T <= H_0`.

*Case 1: `sqrt(K log K/H_0) <= 1/2`, so `gamma = sqrt(K log K/H_0)`.* Then
`2 gamma T + (K log K)/gamma <= 2 gamma H_0 + (K log K)/gamma = 2 sqrt(K H_0 log K) + sqrt(K H_0 log K)
= 3 sqrt(K H_0 log K)`.

*Case 2: `sqrt(K log K/H_0) > 1/2`, so `gamma = 1/2` and `H_0 < 4 K log K`.* Then
`2 gamma T + (K log K)/gamma = T + 2 K log K <= H_0 + 2 K log K`, and
`H_0 = sqrt(H_0) sqrt(H_0) < sqrt(H_0) 2 sqrt(K log K) = 2 sqrt(K H_0 log K) <= 3 sqrt(K H_0 log K)`.

In both cases the loss is at most `3 sqrt(K H_0 log K) + 2 K log K = R_K(H_0)`. `QED`

Two remarks. First, `R_K` is exactly the manuscript's constant
(`appendix_private.tex`: `R_K(H) = 3 sqrt(KH log K) + 2 K log K`), so Lemma A is a strict extension
of `lem:selected-reactive-exp3` and nothing in the manuscript has to change. Second, the lemma needs
only the *selected* agent's conditional mean; no counterfactual reward is required for unselected
agents, which is what makes it usable when agents react to being selected.

## 2.4 Lemma B: the doubling sum, with the absolute constant pinned

**Lemma B.** Run the epoch schedule of Section 2.1 for a total of `V >= 1` visits. The number of
epochs started is `m = ceil( log_2 (V+1) )`, and
```
sum_{k=0}^{m-1} R_K(2^k)  <=  3 (2 + sqrt 2) sqrt( K (V+1) log K )  +  2 K log K log_2( 2(V+1) )
                          <=  11 sqrt( K (V+1) log K )  +  2 K log K log_2( 2(V+1) )
                          =:  R^dbl_K(V) ,
```
with `R^dbl_K(0) := 0`. The absolute constant is `3(2+sqrt 2) = 10.2426...`, and `11` is a round
upper bound for it.

*Proof.* Epochs `0, ..., m-1` cover `2^0 + ... + 2^{m-1} = 2^m - 1` local visits, so the number of
epochs started is the least `m` with `2^m - 1 >= V`, that is `m = ceil(log_2(V+1))`; in particular
`2^m <= 2^{log_2(V+1) + 1} = 2(V+1)` and `m <= log_2(V+1) + 1 = log_2(2(V+1))`. For the square-root
part, `sum_{k=0}^{m-1} 3 sqrt(K 2^k log K) = 3 sqrt(K log K) sum_{k=0}^{m-1} (sqrt 2)^k
= 3 sqrt(K log K) ((sqrt 2)^m - 1)/(sqrt 2 - 1) <= 3 (sqrt 2 + 1) sqrt(K log K) 2^{m/2}
<= 3 (sqrt 2 + 1) sqrt 2 sqrt( K (V+1) log K ) = 3(2 + sqrt 2) sqrt( K (V+1) log K )`.
The additive part is `2 K log K` per epoch, contributing `2 K log K m`. `QED`

## 2.5 Lemma C: the pathwise per-visit honest comparator

**Lemma C.** Assume (H3) and (H4). Fix a cell `C` that is visited at least once, fix any
representative `x_C in C`, let `h_C in K_H` attain `b(x_C)` (any fixed tie-break), and put
```
v_C := inf_{X in C} mu_{h_C}( x_{h_C} ) .
```
Then (i) `b(X) <= v_C + 2 L sqrt d h_n` for every `X in C`, and (ii) whenever `h_C` is selected at a
round whose context lies in `C`, its conditional reward mean is at least `v_C`:
`E[ Y_t | H_{t-1}, I_t = h_C ] >= v_C`.

*Proof.* (i) If `X, X'` lie in the same cube of side `h_n` in `[0,1]^D`, then each block satisfies
`|x_i - x'_i| <= sqrt d h_n`, since a block occupies `d` of the `D` coordinates. Hence
`|mu_h(x_h) - mu_h(x'_h)| <= L sqrt d h_n` for every agent `h`, and, `b` being a maximum of such
functions, `|b(X) - b(X')| <= L sqrt d h_n`. Therefore, for `X in C`,
```
b(X) <= b(x_C) + L sqrt d h_n = mu_{h_C}( x_{h_C,C} ) + L sqrt d h_n ,
```
while for every `X' in C`, `mu_{h_C}(x'_{h_C}) >= mu_{h_C}(x_{h_C,C}) - L sqrt d h_n`, so
`v_C >= mu_{h_C}(x_{h_C,C}) - L sqrt d h_n`. Combining the two displays gives
`b(X) <= v_C + 2 L sqrt d h_n`.
(ii) By honesty, `m_{h_C,t} >= mu_{h_C}(x_{h_C,t}) >= v_C` pointwise, and
`E[Y_t | H_{t-1}, I_t = h_C] = E[ m_{h_C,t} | H_{t-1}, I_t = h_C ]`. `QED`

`h_C` is determined by the instance and the grid alone, before any play, so it is a legitimate fixed
comparator for Lemma A. Note what Lemma C does *not* need: no cell law, no i.i.d. contexts, no
cell-average targets, no fixed ex ante maps. This is the step at which the manuscript's
`lem:cell-law` (contract Sections 11 and 12) is *eliminated* rather than weakened, and it is the
reason Part 2 survives arbitrary contexts and arbitrary behavior.

## 2.6 Theorem 3

**Theorem 3 (Grid-EXP3 robustness under arbitrary behavior and arbitrary oblivious contexts).**
Assume (H1) to (H4). Then for every `n >= 1`, Grid-EXP3 satisfies
```
P(n)  >=  sum_{t=1}^{n} b(x_t)  -  2 L sqrt d n h_n  -  sum_{C} R^dbl_K( V_C ) ,
```
where `V_C` is the number of rounds with `x_t in C`. With the grid of Section 2.1
(`h_n = 1/ceil(n^{1/(D+2)})`) this gives
```
P(n)  >=  sum_{t=1}^{n} b(x_t)
          -  [ 2 L sqrt d + 11 sqrt 2 * 2^{D/2} sqrt(K log K) ] n^{(D+1)/(D+2)}
          -  2^{D+1} K log K * n^{D/(D+2)} log_2( 2(n+1) )
       =  sum_{t=1}^{n} b(x_t)  -  O( n^{(D+1)/(D+2)} sqrt(K log K) )
```
for fixed `D`, `d` and `L`. The bound holds uniformly over every profile allowed by (H2), that is
over every nonanticipating behavioral profile in which the honest agents are honest and the
remaining agents are arbitrary, and uniformly over every oblivious context sequence.

*Proof.*

*Step 1 (contexts are frozen).* Under (H1) the sequence `x_1, ..., x_n` is deterministic. Hence
every cell membership indicator `1{x_t in C}` is deterministic, every visit count `V_C` is a
deterministic integer, the round index at which cell `C` records its `s`th visit is a deterministic
integer, and therefore every epoch of every cell begins at a deterministic global round and has a
deterministic realized length `T_{C,k} = min{ 2^k, (V_C - 2^k + 1)_+ } <= 2^k`. For random exogenous
contexts independent of the platform's randomization and of the reward seeds, condition on the
realized context sequence: the conditional law of the platform's coins and of the reward seeds is
unchanged, every strategy allowed by (H2) remains allowed under the conditioning (the class only
grows, since agents were already permitted to see the whole sequence), the display above holds
conditionally, and integrating over the context law preserves it with `sum_t b(x_t)` replaced by
`E sum_t b(X_t)` and `V_C` by its realization.

*Step 2 (per epoch).* Fix a cell `C` with `V_C >= 1` and an epoch `k` of that cell, with realized
length `T_{C,k}`. Index its local rounds `s = 1, ..., T_{C,k}` and let `t_s` be the corresponding
global round. Set `G_s := H_{t_s}` and `G_0 := H_{t_1 - 1}`. Then, by construction of Grid-EXP3 and
by (H2):

- `p_s` is a function of `w_C` at the start of local round `s`, hence of the epoch's earlier local
  rounds only, so `p_s` is `G_{s-1}`-measurable, and `p_{i,s} >= gamma_k/K`;
- `I_{t_s}` is drawn from `p_s` with fresh platform randomness independent of `G_{s-1}` and of the
  round-`t_s` Bernoulli seed;
- `r_{i,s} := E[ Y_{t_s} | G_{s-1}, I_{t_s} = i ] in [0,1]` is well defined, because the selected
  agent's target is `H_{t_s - 1} v sigma(I_{t_s})`-measurable and the seed is fresh;
- by Lemma C (ii), `r_{h_C, s} >= v_C` for every `s`.

Lemma A applies with `H_0 = 2^k`, `T = T_{C,k}`, comparator `j = h_C` and `v = v_C`, conditionally on
`G_0`, and therefore also after integrating over `G_0`:
```
E[ sum over epoch (C,k) of Y_t ]  >=  v_C T_{C,k}  -  R_K(2^k) .
```

*Step 3 (per cell).* Summing over the `m_C = ceil(log_2(V_C+1))` epochs of cell `C`, whose realized
lengths add up to `V_C`, and applying Lemma B,
```
E[ sum_{t : x_t in C} Y_t ]  >=  v_C V_C  -  R^dbl_K(V_C) .
```

*Step 4 (Lipschitz comparison and summation).* Summing over cells and applying Lemma C (i) pointwise
at each visit,
```
P(n) = sum_C E[ sum_{t : x_t in C} Y_t ]  >=  sum_C v_C V_C  -  sum_C R^dbl_K(V_C)
     >=  sum_C sum_{t : x_t in C} ( b(x_t) - 2 L sqrt d h_n )  -  sum_C R^dbl_K(V_C)
     =   sum_{t<=n} b(x_t)  -  2 L sqrt d n h_n  -  sum_C R^dbl_K(V_C) ,
```
which is the first display.

*Step 5 (rates).* Let `J'_n <= min{J_n, n}` be the number of cells with `V_C >= 1`. Since
`ceil(a) <= 2a` for `a >= 1`, `b_n <= 2 n^{1/(D+2)}` and hence
`h_n <= n^{-1/(D+2)}` and `J_n = b_n^D <= 2^D n^{D/(D+2)}`. By Cauchy and Schwarz,
```
sum_C sqrt(V_C + 1) <= sqrt( J'_n sum_C (V_C + 1) ) = sqrt( J'_n (n + J'_n) ) <= sqrt( 2 J'_n n )
```
using `J'_n <= n`, so `sum_C 11 sqrt(K (V_C+1) log K) <= 11 sqrt 2 sqrt(K J'_n n log K)
<= 11 sqrt 2 * 2^{D/2} sqrt(K log K) n^{(D+1)/(D+2)}`. The logarithmic part of `R^dbl_K` sums to at
most `2 K log K J'_n log_2(2(n+1)) <= 2^{D+1} K log K n^{D/(D+2)} log_2(2(n+1))`. Finally
`2 L sqrt d n h_n <= 2 L sqrt d n^{(D+1)/(D+2)}`. Since `D/(D+2) < (D+1)/(D+2)`, the logarithmic
term is `o( n^{(D+1)/(D+2)} )`. `QED`

**Corollary 3 (occupied cells).** Every occurrence of `J_n` above may be replaced by `J'_n`, the
number of cells that the realized context sequence actually visits. If the contexts occupy only
`N(h_n)` cells at scale `h_n`, the exploration term is `O( sqrt( K N(h_n) n log K ) )`, so the
constant improves automatically when the sequence concentrates. The exponent does not improve unless
`h_n` is retuned to a known covering dimension, which the mechanism cannot do without that
knowledge. (This is the analogue of `MATH_CANDIDATE_LEDGER.md` Section 5 item (a) for Grid-EXP3.)

## 2.7 What Theorem 3 does not give

Stated bluntly, because the result is easy to over-sell.

1. **No monotonicity.** None is claimed and none is proved. The manuscript's contextual monotonicity
   proofs (`appendix_contextual.tex`) run an index race on fixed per-cell means supplied by
   `lem:cell-law`; Grid-EXP3 has no fixed per-cell means, the selection probabilities depend on all
   agents' realized rewards jointly through a shared normalization, and under (H2) there is no
   fixed-mean reduction to couple. I did not attempt a pathwise coupling and I do not conjecture one
   either way.
2. **No fairness among top agents.** Under (H2) two agents that are both top need not have
   exchangeable behavior, so the symmetry argument that gives exact expected FATA for the
   manuscript's rules is unavailable.
3. **No sharp adaptivity.** The theorem bounds the platform's total reward, not the selected
   effort of a given agent on a region, and the manuscript's contextual sharp adaptivity is a
   per-agent, per-region statement that the argument above does not produce.
4. **No equilibrium and no incentive content at all.** Explicit exploration gives every agent at
   least `gamma_k / K` selection probability at every cell visit, so an agent that always delivers
   `0` still collects a positive fraction of the pulls. Grid-EXP3 does not incentivize effort; it
   only floors the platform's reward. Any use of this result must say so in the same sentence.
5. **The benchmark is the honest intrinsic benchmark `sum_t b(x_t)`**, not `sum_t M_star(x_t)` and
   not second-capacity revenue. Nothing about revenue is claimed.
6. **Fixed horizon.** `h_n` depends on `n`. An anytime version would need an outer doubling over the
   global horizon with a fresh grid per outer epoch (`MATH_CANDIDATE_LEDGER.md` Section 5 item (c));
   that is not proved here.
7. **Oblivious contexts only.** (H1) requires the context sequence to be independent of the
   mechanism's realized randomness. An *adapted* adversary that chooses `x_t` after seeing the
   platform's past coins is **not** covered: Step 1 then fails, the realized epoch lengths become
   random and correlated with the play, and Lemma A is stated for a deterministic `T`. Extending to
   adapted contexts would require an optional-stopping version of `(*)`, which I did not prove. The
   manuscript's own `lem:selected-reactive-exp3` avoids the same difficulty by assuming `H` known at
   restart.
8. **Lipschitz intrinsic means are required.** (H3) is used only through `b` and only in Lemma C,
   but it is used, and no claim is made for merely measurable `mu_i`. Capacities are unrestricted.
9. **Constants are exponential in `D = K d`.** The factor `2^{D/2}` in Step 5 comes from the ceiling
   in `b_n`; the result is asymptotic in `n` with `K` and `d` fixed.
10. **It is not a strict improvement on `thm:grid-ucb-robust`.** It matches that theorem's exponent
    and in fact removes its `(log n)^{1/(D+2)}` factor, replacing `log n` by `log K` inside the
    square root, while weakening i.i.d. contexts to oblivious contexts and `S_HDS` to arbitrary
    behavior. But `thm:grid-ucb-robust` additionally delivers exact monotonicity, expected FATA,
    sharp adaptivity and a public equilibrium, none of which Theorem 3 has. The honest description is
    "same rate for the robustness half, under far weaker assumptions, at the cost of everything
    else".

## 2.8 Least secure step in Part 2

**Step 1.** Freezing the context sequence and concluding that every epoch begins at a deterministic
round with a deterministic realized length is what lets Lemma A be applied with a deterministic `T`.
I believe it, and it is the standard reduction used by `MATH_CANDIDATE_LEDGER.md` Section 4 step 1
for `C2`, but it is exactly the hypothesis that breaks for adapted context adversaries (Section 2.7
item 7), and it is the clause a referee will test first. If a reader wants adapted contexts, the
result as proved does not cover them.

Second least secure: the filtration bookkeeping in Step 2, specifically the claim that
`r_{i,s} = E[Y_{t_s} | G_{s-1}, I_{t_s} = i]` is well defined and that
`E[hatY_{i,s} | G_{s-1}] = r_{i,s}` when the agent's target may depend on `I_{t_s}` and on the whole
context sequence. This is the "selected reactive" content of the manuscript's lemma and I reproduced
its structure, but it deserves a second reader. Third: the constant `3(2+sqrt 2)` of Lemma B, which
I verified by hand twice but which is easy to get wrong by a factor of `sqrt 2` depending on whether
`2^{m/2}` is bounded by `sqrt(2(V+1))` or by `sqrt(V+1)`.
