# Baselines and ablations for the scalar and contextual experiments (SCOUT A3, 17 September 2026)

Read-only scouting report for Agent A. Nothing in the repository was edited; no experiment was run beyond one-minute sanity checks on the login node (listed in Section 7 and Section 9). Every external mechanism described below was checked against a primary source on 17 September 2026; the URLs are in Section 8. Numbers quoted from the retained suite come from `scratch/experiments/final/*/trials.csv` as regenerated at baseline.

Conventions. "S-star" is MESP's proposed profile (Section 5 of the paper: capacity in bidding and fallback, `min(tau, M_i)` for a strategic primary winner, `max(mu_i, min(tau, M_i))` for an honest one). "All-max" is the profile in which every agent delivers `M_i` in every round. "Hand-picked deviations" are the seven behaviors in `scratch/experiments/final/scalar_incentives/run.py` lines 43 to 47 (underbid 0.05 and 0.15, primary shade 0.05 and 0.15, bait-and-switch, trigger-fallback shade, non-top win then underperform; the last two set identical targets, `BASELINE.md` lead 1). "BR search" is the black-box best-response search another scout is designing; its output is, for every mechanism, a lower bound on exploitability within the searched class and never a proof of equilibrium or of its absence.

Two kinds of comparison are used throughout and must be labelled in every figure and table:

- Type P ("same behavior, different mechanism"): fix one agent behavior profile and run every mechanism on it. Fair as a performance comparison. Meaningful behaviors: all-max; S-star restricted to the mechanisms that announce a price (for a mechanism without a price, `min(tau, M_i)` is undefined, so S-star cannot be imposed on plain UCB); each hand-picked deviation by the focal agent with the others at all-max or S-star.
- Type M ("each mechanism under its own best tested response"): for each mechanism, evaluate the deviator's gain over that mechanism's own proposed profile for every tested deviation and for the BR search, and report the maximum. This is the mechanism-design-relevant comparison; it compares exploitability lower bounds, not equilibria.

## 1. Summary and recommended minimal set

Findings that shape the recommendation.

1. The scalar performance suite already contains four benchmarks (`ucb_capacity`, `ucb_intrinsic`, `uniform_capacity`, `oracle_second_price`), but none of them is a private-information mechanism, so the figure currently shows MESP paying a price against learners that are handed the answer (agents at capacity) or that ignore incentives. What is missing is the family of empirical-second-price mechanisms that differ from MESP by one component each. Those are simultaneously the natural baselines and the ablation ladder; they should be one experiment.
2. The correct "earlier rule" from Esmaeili, Shin, Slivkins (AAAI 2025) is not a single object. Their SP+SAMF (Algorithm 1) elicits bids by one pull per arm, announces the second highest value `m'`, runs a SAMF algorithm (UCB) over all arms and blocks an arm that bid at most `m'` but later delivers a reward above `m'` (it punishes untruthfully low bids, not shortfall), then ends with a short reward phase. Exclusion on shortfall is their Pure-SP (Algorithm 2, modelled on Braverman et al. 2019), which they prove non-robust (Theorem 7.1). Section 4 gives both, verified on the arXiv full text, with faithful noisy adaptations. The noisy Pure-SP adaptation coincides with the ablation rung "monitoring with permanent exclusion"; the noisy SP+SAMF adaptation is a separate baseline whose incentive properties are unproven under noise rather than known to fail.
3. The theorem's additive slack is numerically large at the tested horizons. At `n = 20,000`, `K = 3`: `L_n = 1,634` (bidding uses 24.5 percent of the horizon), `s_n = 0.113`, `b_n(N_n)/N_n = 0.037`, `R_K(N_n)/N_n = 0.045`; the per-round slack `L_n/n + 2 s_n + 2 b_n(n)/n + R_K(n)/n` is about 0.41, larger than any deviation gain that can physically occur (utility per round lies in [0, 2]). At `n = 10^6` it is still about 0.11. Consequently a BR search will find positive finite-horizon gains against MESP (boundary-riding shading of order `b_n(r)/r`, and underbidding worth `delta L_n / n`), and they are consistent with the theorem. Every baseline table must carry the theorem slack alongside the measured gains so that reviewers read the gains correctly.
4. The paired-difference spread differs by an order of magnitude between instances. In `unique_top` the focal agent always wins Stage 1 (focal_won = 1.000 in all 840 rows at `n = 20,000`), so per-round paired gains have standard deviation 0.003 to 0.04 and 120 to 240 replications give half-widths of a few thousandths. In `tied_top` and `low_gap_nontop` the focal agent wins with probability 0.508 and 0.362, and the paired difference mixes two regimes; its standard deviation is 0.10 to 0.19 per round and about 10^4 replications would be needed for a 0.002 half-width. The proof partitions on the winning event; the experiment should too (report `P(win)` and the gain conditional on the coupled winning event separately, or couple Stage-1 rewards through common uniforms so that the deviating win event is pathwise contained in the baseline win event, as in Appendix C.3 of the paper). This is a design point for the statistics scout; I record it here because it decides whether the tied-top rungs of the ladder are estimable at all.
5. Two implementation facts matter for the ablations. (a) The current `ucb_capacity` baseline and both contextual UCB classes use a fixed initialization order and lowest-index tie-breaking (`np.argmax`, `unseen[0]`), whereas Theorems 3 (scalar UCB) and 6 (finite-context UCB) are stated for uniform random initialization and uniform tie-breaking, and the FATA claim is "exact". The symmetric variant must be the reference and the fixed-order variant the ablation, not the other way round. (b) The scalar incentives code generates Stage-1 successes with `rng.binomial(ell, bid_m)`, which does not couple monotonically across behaviors; the coupling used in the proof (`Y = 1{U <= m}`) is cheap to implement and removes a variance source.

Boundary-riding deviation (new hand-picked behavior, needed by R2 and R3). The winner delivers `m_r = max(0, tau - c / sqrt(r))` on its `r`-th primary pull, with `c` chosen so that `sum_r (tau - m_r) = (1 - eps) b_n(N_n)`; equivalently, it shades by a constant `0.05` for the first `floor((1 - eps) log(2 n^4) / (2 * 0.05^2))` pulls (about 8,000 at `n = 20,000`) and then returns to `tau`. Both versions save about `(1 - eps) b_n(N_n)` utility (about 550 units, 0.028 per round at `n = 20,000`) and trigger the monitor with probability close to the false-switch probability. This is the natural exploit of any cumulative monitor and its gain is exactly the theorem's `2 b_n(n)` term; it should be in the menu so that the BR search does not appear to have discovered something the theory missed.

## 4. The Esmaeili, Shin, Slivkins rule: what the original does, and a noisy adaptation

Verification. Full text checked on the arXiv HTML version (`https://arxiv.org/html/2312.07929`, downloaded with curl on 17 September 2026 and searched; arXiv title "Robust and Performance Incentivizing Algorithms for Multi-Armed Bandits with Strategic Agents") and the AAAI proceedings record (`https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/33510`, Vol. 39 No. 13, pages 13814 to 13822, DOI 10.1609/aaai.v39i13.33510, published 11 April 2025, title without "and"). The bibliography entry `esmaeili` in `references.bib` matches the AAAI record; adding the DOI is advisable.

Their model (Section 3.1 of the arXiv version, quoted). "At round t, the algorithm chooses/pulls an arm (agent) I_t and a reward r_{I_t}(t) in [0,1] is sampled from the static distribution D_{I_t} whose mean is mu_{I_t}. After seeing the reward, arm I_t can modify the reward by adding a value of effort c_{I_t}(t) leading to the final reward r~ = r + c. Note that the algorithm only observes the reward r~." With `Support(D_i) subset [0, M_i]` and `c_i(t) <= M_i - r_i(t)`. So the base reward is random, but the arm sees the realization before choosing the delivered value, and the platform observes a value the arm controls exactly (up to feasibility). That is why one pull can serve as a bid and why "gives a value less than m'" is an exact test. Utility `u_i = E T_i(n) - E C_i(n)`; honest agents have `c_i(t) >= 0`; blind observation model (arms do not see the round index or others' pulls). Equilibrium is a ratio notion, `lim u_i(S_i', S_{-i}) / u_i(S) <= 1`, and a "partial" version.

SP+SAMF, Algorithm 1 (Section 6), verbatim structure:
1. Pull each arm once; `m_i` is its reported value.
2. `m'` is the second highest reported value.
3. Tell all arms `m'`.
4. Ignore all previous rewards and run a SAMF MAB algorithm (UCB is their example) with two modifications: 4A, if an arm with `m_i <= m'` gives a reward `r > m'`, block it and never play it again; 4B, stop at round `n - k ceil(ln n)^(rho + 3)`.
5. Reward phase: pull each arm `i` for `N_i` rounds with `E N_i = mu_hat_i^SAMF (ln n)^(rho + 3)`, where `mu_hat_i^SAMF` is its mean during the SAMF phase.

Theorem 6.1: the profile in which every arm bids truthfully, arms with `M_i <= m'` give `M_i` in the SAMF phase, an arm with `M_i > m'` gives `m' + 1/ln n`, and rewards are absorbed (strategic) or unmodified (honest) in the reward phase, is an asymptotic equilibrium. Theorem 6.2: equilibrium revenue `M_{top-1} n - o(n)` and revenue `mu_H^* n - o(n)` under any profile (because the SAMF phase is sharply adaptive and honest agents bid truthfully). Appendix C explains rule 4A: without it, the unique top arm can bid low so that `m'` drops to the third value, then overdeliver slightly above the runner-up and collect `n - o(n)` pulls at a lower price. So 4A punishes untruthfully low bids that are later contradicted, not shortfall.

Pure-SP, Algorithm 2 (Section 7, "a mechanism similar to the one introduced in Braverman et al. 2019"): one-pull bids; announce `m'`; cycle among the arms that bid the maximum; 5A, delete an arm if it gives a value less than `m'`; stop at `n - 2k`; reward phase with `E N_i = 2 m_i`. Theorem 7.1: there is an all-honest instance with truthful bids in which a per-pull deviation of probability `epsilon > 0` makes revenue `o(n)` with probability at least `1 - 1/n`. This is the "immediate exclusion on shortfall" rule the lead's brief refers to; in the original it is the negative example, not the proposal.

Our paper's description. `Private_information_setting.tex` line 5 says the earlier construction "observes a directly chosen reward"; that is accurate in the sense above (chosen after the realization). `Public_information_setting.tex` line 18 says "noisy monitoring cannot by itself justify permanent exclusion", which is a statement about the Pure-SP style rule and about 4A; it should name which rule is meant when the related-work text is revised.

Noisy adaptation A (noisy Pure-SP, equals ablation rung R6 variant "permanent exclusion"). Replace the single revealing pull by `L_n` pulls and the empirical bid `beta_i`; announce `tau = (beta_(2) - s_n)_+`; pull the top bidder with the anytime monitor; on a switch, delete it permanently and promote the next bidder at `(beta_(3) - s_n)_+` with a fresh monitor; last agent unmonitored; drop the reward phase (its role, rewarding non-winners proportionally to their bids, is `o(n)` and only affects non-top incentives in the deterministic proof). Why it can fail under noise: exclusion on a single sub-price outcome (the literal 5A) fires on the first zero outcome, which for a compliant winner delivering `tau` happens at pull 1 with probability `1 - tau` (simulation above: 399 of 400 compliant runs switched under the no-slack test, all under the literal single-pull rule); exclusion on an anytime empirical test fixes false exclusion (probability at most `(2 n^3)^(-1)`) but then any exclusion is permanent, so the honest benchmark is guaranteed only up to that probability and the tie-top revenue is lost after a switch. The remaining defect is not statistical but structural: the mechanism has no answer once the last bidder is reached.

Noisy adaptation B (noisy SP+SAMF). Stage 1 as MESP; announce `m' = beta_(2)` (or the conservative `(beta_(2) - s_n)_+`); Stage 2 symmetric UCB over all `K` agents with fresh statistics and the blocking test 4A': block agent `i` with `beta_i <= m'` if after `T` Stage-2 pulls its empirical mean exceeds `m' + sqrt(log(2 K n^4) / (2 T))`; 4B' and a reward phase of `K ceil(ln n)^(rho + 3)` rounds proportional to Stage-2 means. What is faithful: bids by pulls, the announced second value, UCB in the long phase, the blocking of contradicted low bids. Why it can fail or cannot be certified under noise: (i) the literal 4A fires on any success because every Bernoulli success is a reward above `m' < 1`; (ii) with the vanishing margin, an honest agent whose bid underestimated its capacity (in a tied-top instance the losing top agent's bid is below its capacity with probability about one half, and then `m' = beta_h < M_h`) is blocked after about `log(2 K n^4) / (2 (M_h - beta_h)^2)`, of order `L_n`, pulls, so the honest comparator is lost with constant probability exactly in the two-top case; the conservative `m' = beta_(2) - s_n` repairs this at the cost of an `s_n n` revenue term, which is the same term MESP pays; (iii) with a non-vanishing margin the Appendix C exploit returns within the margin; (iv) even when the tests are tuned, the equilibrium argument of Theorem 6.1 needs UCB's response to a unique top arm that delivers `m' + 1/ln n` (gap `1/ln n`, so `O((ln n)^3)` pulls to the others, still `o(n)`) and needs sharp adaptivity of UCB against phase-dependent targets, which in the noisy model is proven in our paper only for `S_HI`. So adaptation B is a legitimate baseline whose incentive guarantee is open, not a rule known to be broken; the honest framing is "the estimation-versus-exploitation trade-off in 4A' and the unproven behavioral robustness of the UCB phase are the reasons MESP monitors a single winner and falls back to EXP3".

## 7. Cost estimates

Measured on the login node with the venv (`n = 20,000`, `K = 3`, single core): vectorized Stage 2 (Bernoulli stream, cumulative sum, first crossing) 0.23 ms per episode; the Python EXP3 loop 8.8 microseconds per round; a Python UCB loop 4.1 microseconds per round. The retained suite took 22.5 minutes on 4 cores (`BASELINE.md`).

- MESP, ESP-NoMon, ESP-Excl, R1, R2 and the no-slack and windowed monitors of R3: about 1 ms per episode when no fallback runs, 0.15 s when an EXP3 fallback of 16,000 rounds runs (bait-and-switch, shading). A cell of 240 paired replications times 10 behaviors times 3 instances times 4 horizons is under one CPU hour even if every episode falls back.
- ESP-UCBfb and UCB-based rungs: 4 to 9 microseconds per fallback round, same order as EXP3.
- Noisy SP+SAMF: a full-horizon Python UCB loop (about 0.1 s per episode at `n = 20,000`, 0.25 s at 50,000); 240 replications times 6 behaviors times 4 horizons is about 1 CPU hour.
- Contextual additions at `K = 3`, `D in {3, 6}`: the current suite spends about 480 s of learner time for 6,600 method runs; adding one method and one agent raises this by about 1.5 times; Grid-UCB in the incentives suite doubles that suite (about 4 minutes on 3 workers today).
- The BR search is out of scope here; note that the black box must be given the mechanism as a function of the deviator's policy, so all the variants above should be implemented behind one interface (`mechanism(config, policy, seed)`) to avoid re-implementing the search per rung.

Replications, order of magnitude (details are the statistics scout's). Performance contrasts (Type P, all-max or S-star) have paired standard deviations of about 0.01 per round at `n = 20,000` and 0.02 to 0.03 at 2,000, so 120 to 200 paired replications give 95 percent half-widths of 0.002 to 0.005 against effects of 0.02 to 0.33. Incentive contrasts in `unique_top` need 120 to 240. Tied and low-gap instances need either conditioning on the coupled winning event (then 240 suffice) or of order 10^4 unconditional replications; recommend the former. False-switch frequencies for the anytime monitor cannot be estimated (theory bounds them by `(2 n^3)^(-1)`); report them as "0 of R" with the theoretical bound, and estimate them only for the ablated monitors where they are of order 1.
