# Real-data and application options for the non-synthetic track (SCOUT A1)

Date of assessment: 17 September 2026. All URLs below were fetched on this date with WebFetch, WebSearch, or git/curl from the login node; every fetch is listed in Section 5 with what it confirmed. No dataset was downloaded. Nothing under `sections/`, `references.bib`, `figures/`, or `scratch/experiments/final/` was touched. Evidence categories used throughout: (RD) real deployment evidence; (OL) offline evaluation on logged data; (RB) replay of precomputed real outcomes under scripted agent policies (a category between OL and CS: labels, provider answers and outcomes are real and no logging propensities are involved, but the strategic choice of effort is scripted, not observed); (CS) real-data-calibrated simulation; (SS) fully synthetic simulation.

## 1. Summary and ranked shortlist

No public dataset contains repeated identifiable agents, contextual tasks, verifiable outcomes, and an observed or manipulated effort level all at once. Crowd-label corpora (CIFAR-10H, CIFAR-10N, the Toloka and TREC relevance sets, the Snow et al. RTE and temporal sets, Bluebirds) supply real accuracy heterogeneity across workers and items but record no effort and no effort intervention, so they can calibrate intrinsic means and, only under a stated assumption, capacities. Human-subject studies that did manipulate incentives or time limits (Ho et al. 2015, Maddalena et al. 2016, DellaVigna and Pope 2018, the two PLOS ONE pay studies, the 2024 view-time-limit study) either have no public worker-level data that I could verify or observe each worker on a single task without contexts. Expert-routing logs (Stack Exchange, oDesk, clinical records) are self-selected or access-restricted and identify no counterfactual allocation. The strongest option that meets the model is therefore a provider testbed in which real labeled tasks are answered by algorithmic providers whose computation is a controllable knob: outcomes are real, capacity and intrinsic mean have operational definitions measured from the knob-to-accuracy curve, provider-local contexts are available, and every counterfactual (any provider, any knob, any task) is queryable, so no propensity correction is needed. Two instantiations are recommended, sharing one simulation harness built around a precomputed "response bank": (1) scikit-learn ensembles on CC BY 4.0 or MIT tabular and image datasets with the number of evaluated trees or boosting stages as the compute knob (fully local, cheap, large task pools); (2) open-weight LLM samples from the MIT-licensed Monkey Business release (10,000 real samples per problem for several Llama-3 and Gemma models on GSM8K and MATH subsets) with the number of self-consistency votes as the compute knob (no inference required, no paid API). Option (3), a crowd-data-calibrated simulator on CIFAR-10H, is the recommended realization of the user's second preference and must be presented with its fitted capacity assumption stated as such.

## 3. Shortlisted candidates in detail

### 3.1 Option 1: scikit-learn provider bank with a compute knob (evidence category RB)

Goal. Instantiate the model with real labeled tasks, real providers, and a real, controllable computation knob, while keeping the strategic choice scripted.

Data. Primary: Fashion-MNIST (MIT; 60,000 train, 10,000 test, 10 classes). Secondary: UCI Covertype (CC BY 4.0; 581,012 rows, 54 features, 7 classes) for a large tabular pool, and UCI Letter Recognition (CC BY 4.0; 20,000 rows, 26 classes). All three licenses were read on the source pages (Section 5). OpenML `mnist_784` reports licence "Public", which is ambiguous; do not rely on it.

Providers (agents). K in {3, 4, 5} providers, each a scikit-learn ensemble frozen after training. Heterogeneity is created by training data, not by tampering with predictions: for example provider 1 trained on a class-skewed subset, provider 2 on a feature-region-skewed subset, provider 3 on a small uniform subset, provider 4 a different family (HistGradientBoosting with `staged_predict`), provider 5 an extra-trees forest. Each forest has T = 256 or 512 trees.

Effort knob and cost. For a forest provider the knob is k, the number of trees evaluated at inference, drawn uniformly without replacement from the T trained trees; the majority vote of those k trees is the answer. Compute cost is proportional to k and is recorded. For a boosting provider the knob is the number of stages used (`staged_predict`), which is also linear in cost. To reach a target mean below the cheapest honest level, the provider answers deliberately wrongly with a scripted probability (sabotage), which is the operational meaning of negative effort in the model. Any target m in [0, M_i(x)] is reached by mixing two knob levels (or a knob level with sabotage), so the attainable set is the interval the model assumes even if the raw knob-to-accuracy curve is not perfectly monotone in k.

Operational definitions. Fix a knob menu k in {k_1 < k_2 < ... < k_J}, for example {1, 2, 4, 8, 16, 32, 64, 128, 256}. Split the held-out pool into a measurement half and a play half. On the measurement half, estimate A_i(x, k) = P(correct | context cell x, knob k) for each provider and cell by averaging correctness over tasks in the cell and over R fresh random tree subsets per task. Define mu_i(x) := A_i(x, k_1) (accuracy at the cheapest honest level) and M_i(x) := max_j A_i(x, k_j) (largest cell mean attainable within the menu). Then M_i(x) >= mu_i(x) by construction, the menu is public to the experimenter and the "capacity" is explicitly menu-relative (a larger menu gives a larger M). Report the full curves A_i(x, k) in the appendix so that readers see how monotone they actually are; do not smooth them. Play-half outcomes are what the mechanism observes.

Contexts. Provider-local, as the model requires: x_{i,t} is provider i's cheap-pass confidence on the round's task (the vote share of its top class among its first k_1 trees, or the top predicted probability of a frozen depth-limited tree), quantized to a grid in [0,1]; d = 1 and D = K. All K contexts are deterministic functions of the same fresh task, so the profile X_t is i.i.d. with a fixed joint law (the empirical law of the play pool), exogenous, and computed platform-side from frozen models, so agents cannot manipulate it. A second, coarser context (the task's predicted difficulty from a separate frozen model) is a useful ablation because it is identical across providers.

Why outcomes are Bernoulli with the agent's chosen mean. Each round draws a fresh task uniformly from the play pool. Conditional on the cell and on the knob chosen, correctness is a Bernoulli draw with mean A_i(x, k) because the task index is i.i.d.; reuse of a task in a later round does not break independence because the draw is with replacement from the same population. What the platform "observes" is correctness against the stored true label, which simulates a verifiable-outcome setting (gold questions or delayed ground truth). State this.

Scripted policies. Honest: always k = k_1 or a fixed higher k (never sabotage). Proposed profile for MESP: k = k_J in bidding and fallback; in the primary phase the smallest k whose cell mean is at least tau, mixing two adjacent levels if needed. Deviations: the five scalar deviations already in `scratch/experiments/final` (underbid by using a smaller k in bidding, primary shading, bait-and-switch by dropping to k_1 after winning, triggered fallback then shading, non-top winning), translated into knob schedules; for grid rules, ex ante maps x -> k fixed before play.

Harness. A `ResponseBank` object with `sample(provider, task, knob, rng) -> {0,1}` and `cells(task) -> X_t`; the existing MESP, Grid-UCB, Grid-ETC, context-blind UCB, and LinUCB code then calls the bank instead of `Bernoulli(m)`. Two implementations of the bank (A1 forests, A2 LLM samples) run under one experiment script with the same paired-seed protocol and bootstrap intervals as the current suite.

Sizes and budget. Training 5 forests of 256 trees on 30,000 examples of Fashion-MNIST: minutes per forest on 4 cores. Bank precomputation: store per-tree predictions on the play pool (T x tasks integers, about 256 x 10,000 x 1 byte per provider), then any k-subset vote is computed on the fly, so no per-knob storage is needed. Simulation cost is the same order as the present suite (22 minutes wall on 4 cores for everything); with two banks and the same cell design, plan 20 to 60 core-hours including bank measurement, well below the 2,000 budget, no GPU. Storage under 2 GB. Human effort: harness and bank 12 hours; provider training, context and curve measurement 8 hours; policy translation and MESP/grid integration 8 hours; runs, plots, appendix text 12 hours; total about 40 hours, feasible before 28 September if started by 19 September.

Claims this supports (as RB evidence). (i) On real providers with real outcomes, MESP's empirical price tracks the second-largest measured capacity and its revenue per round approaches M_(2) as n grows; (ii) the cumulative monitor detects a scripted bait-and-switch (drop to k_1) within the horizons tested and the deviation is costly in paired utility; (iii) the honest benchmark holds under all scripted profiles; (iv) static-grid rules track cell-wise real mu_i(x) that were never verified to be Lipschitz, with a measured comparison against LinUCB and context-blind UCB; (v) the finite-horizon incentive diagnostics of the paper replicate when Bernoulli(m) is replaced by real correctness draws. Claims it cannot support: equilibrium or exploitability beyond the scripted class; anything about human or real strategic behavior; that provider cost is linear in the delivered mean (the model's c = m minus mu is a convention; report the real compute cost alongside); generality beyond the chosen providers, menus and pools; a Lipschitz constant.

Wording for the paper. "Real labeled tasks and real provider answers; the effort choice is scripted." Never "deployment", never "real strategic agents".

### 3.2 Option 2: LLM provider bank from Monkey Business (evidence category RB)

Data. `ScalingIntelligence/monkey_business` on Hugging Face: samples released under the MIT License (dataset card, verified); fields `question`, `gt_answer`, `prompt`, `samples` (10,000 strings), `is_corrects` (10,000 booleans), `orig_dset_split`, `orig_dset_idx` (verified in README). Problem counts: GSM8K 127 problems (one removed for a wrong ground truth), MATH 128, CodeContests 140, MiniF2F 130 (verified). Sampling: temperature 0.6 for GSM8K and MATH, 512 max tokens, few-shot prompts fixed per task (verified). Grading: regex plus string equality for GSM8K; sympy equivalence for MATH (verified). Files: GSM8K Llama-3-8B-Instruct 367 MB and 70B-Instruct 333 MB; MATH files 656 MB to 852 MB for the Gemma and Llama models (verified in the repository tree). Underlying problems: GSM8K MIT (verified), MATH through `EleutherAI/hendrycks_math` MIT with level and subject fields (verified).

Providers. GSM8K: K = 2 (Llama-3-8B-Instruct, Llama-3-70B-Instruct), which meets the K >= 2 requirement of the private theorem but gives a thin instance. MATH: K up to 5 (Gemma-2B, Gemma-7B, Llama-3-8B, Llama-3-8B-Instruct, Llama-3-70B-Instruct); Pythia files exist but are expected to have near-zero single-sample accuracy and would act as very weak non-top agents (measure before deciding).

Effort knob and outcomes. Knob k = number of samples drawn uniformly without replacement from the 10,000 stored samples; answer = majority over extracted final answers (GSM8K: the number after `####` or the last number in the sample; MATH: the last `\boxed{}` content after light normalization); outcome = correctness of the majority cluster, taken as the majority of the stored `is_corrects` flags within the winning cluster (this avoids re-implementing sympy grading and keeps grading identical to the release). Cost is proportional to k. Sabotage implements means below mu. Menu example k in {1, 2, 3, 5, 8, 13, 21, 34}. Capacity and intrinsic mean are defined exactly as in 3.1 with A_i(x, k) measured on a measurement half of the samples (for example the first 5,000 sample indices) and play on the other half, so measured curves and played outcomes use disjoint real samples.

Contexts. Provider-local: x_{i,t} = agreement indicator or agreement rate among provider i's first two drawn samples for the round's problem (a cheap pass whose two samples are then also part of the k used, so the pass costs nothing extra), d = 1. Task-level alternative for MATH: level 1 to 5 and subject via `orig_dset_idx`, which yields a finite context set matching the finite-profile results of Section 4 of the paper.

Limits to state. The problem pool is 127 or 128 problems, so the "population" is a fixed small subset and cells will contain few problems; the Bernoulli mean per cell mixes problem draws and sample draws; the release fixed temperature and prompts. Report results as "on the Monkey Business 128-problem MATH subset" and never as "on MATH".

Budget. Download 0.7 GB (GSM8K) to about 3.6 GB (five MATH files); parsing 1.28 million strings per file is minutes; bank measurement and simulations under 20 core-hours; storage under 5 GB in scratch. Human effort: about 25 hours on top of the Option 1 harness (download and parsing 6, majority-vote bank and validation against `is_corrects` 6, integration 4, runs and writing 9).

Claims. As in 3.1, for LLM providers with a self-consistency compute knob; additionally a qualitative link to inference-time scaling (coverage and majority accuracy increase with k, as the source paper reports). Not: anything about the API providers of the platform story, or about the full benchmarks.

### 3.3 Option 3: crowd-data-calibrated simulator on CIFAR-10H (evidence category CS)

Data. GitHub `jcpeterson/cifar-10h`, CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 (LICENSE.txt verified); raw per-trial CSV with `annotator_id`, `trial_index`, `is_attn_check`, `true_label`, `chosen_label`, `cifar10_test_set_idx`, `reaction_time`, `time_elapsed` (verified); each annotator completed 200 normal trials plus 10 attention checks and each image received about 50 labels (README, verified). The paper's abstract (arXiv 1908.07086) does not restate the counts; treat 2,571 annotators as the README-level figure to re-verify after download.

What is identifiable. For each worker i and context x, the accuracy mu_i(x) is identifiable from the worker's own trials, where x is a per-image difficulty statistic computed from the other workers' labels (for example one minus the share agreeing with the true label, binned into 5 to 10 levels) or the true class. With 200 trials per worker, per-cell estimates are noisy; fit a two-parameter logistic per worker (accuracy against difficulty) and use a shrinkage prior across workers, and say so. Attention-check trials must be excluded from mu estimation.

What is not identifiable and must be assumed. Capacity M_i(x): the data contain no effort observation and no effort intervention, so any M_i(x) is a fitted assumption. Present at most two transparent choices and label them as assumptions: (a) M_i(x) = 1 minus (1 minus mu_i(x)) times (1 minus lambda) for a stated lambda (effort removes a fraction lambda of errors); (b) M_i(x) = accuracy at x of the top decile of workers (what a careful worker achieves at that difficulty), clipped to be at least mu_i(x). Reaction time is observed but is neither effort nor an intervention; do not present it as evidence about effort (COMMON rule 6). The effort cost, the strategic choice, and the Bernoulli draws are all simulated.

Design. Select K workers with diverse fitted curves (at least two with tied top capacity under the assumption, for the public theorem), run the same MESP and grid experiments as the current synthetic suite with the fitted mu_i(x) and assumed M_i(x). Contexts are i.i.d. because images are drawn i.i.d.

Budget. About 20 person-hours; compute under 10 core-hours; storage under 200 MB. Ethics: none (public, anonymized integer worker ids); share-alike means any released table of fitted per-worker parameters must be licensed CC BY-NC-SA 4.0.

Claims. "Mechanism behavior on intrinsic-mean heterogeneity fitted to real crowd labels, with capacities set by a stated assumption." Not: evidence about effort, about workers' strategic behavior, or about capacities.
