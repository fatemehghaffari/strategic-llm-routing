## 1. Decision in one paragraph

Adopt package P1, five components. (E1) Exact exploitability of MESP over the full behavioral class B_{i,n}: the deviator's one-step objective is affine in its target, so an optimal action is always an endpoint and Stages 1 and 2 reduce to two-action backward inductions over (own pull count, own success count) with no target grid and no discretization error; the fallback phase is sandwiched between a certified lower continuation (zero) and the appendix's own EXP3 bound, giving a certified interval [Exploit_LB, Exploit_UB] per instance and horizon up to n = 50,000; a CMA-ES search over bang-bang threshold policies is kept only as an independent check (it must not exceed Exploit_UB) and as a searched lower bound for the fallback POMDP. Scout A2's login-node pilot already gives, on the unique-top instance, certified per-round brackets [0.125, 0.175] at n = 500, [0.108, 0.151] at 1,000, [0.091, 0.120] at 2,000, against 0.026 for the best hand-picked deviation in the current data; the gain decomposes into adaptive underbidding worth about (M_i minus V_i) L_n and boundary tracking worth b_n(N_n) to within one utility unit, both terms the theorem already pays for. (E2) A mechanism baseline and ablation suite built from empirical-second-price variants that differ from MESP by one component (no monitor, round-robin fallback, permanent exclusion, UCB fallback, warm start, uncorrected price, shorter bidding), evaluated both as "same behavior, different mechanism" and as "each mechanism under its own best tested response", with joint adversarial profiles for the honest-benchmark claim. (E3) Exact in-class exploitability for the fixed-map theorems: S_HDS for Static Grid-ETC by a per-cell one-dimensional computation on a theorem-compliant two-component instance (top set {1,2} on x_3 < 0.6 and {1,2,3} on x_3 > 0.6), extended to n = 2 x 10^6 at trivial cost, with count- and phase-dependent stress tests reported in a separate hatched panel labeled outside S_HDS; and S_HI for public UCB, tied versus unique top. (E4) One non-synthetic evaluation: a scikit-learn provider bank on Fashion-MNIST (MIT) and UCI Covertype (CC BY 4.0) in which frozen real providers answer real labeled tasks with the number of trees or boosting stages as a controllable compute knob, so outcomes are real and only the strategic effort choice is scripted. (E5) Regeneration of the existing suites with the defects repaired (tape coupling, one mechanism module, one instance table, symmetric initialization, seed phases, t and BCa intervals, false-switch column, single-process runtime). The main paper gets about half a page and one four-panel figure; everything else goes to the supplement. Pilots with go/no-go criteria run 18 to 20 September; final runs 21 to 25 September; no run after 28 September; total compute about 300 core-hours of the 2,000 available and no GPU.

Why P1 ranks first. It answers all four criticisms in the brief: synthetic dependence (E4), no exploitability study (E1, E3), no mechanism baselines or ablations (E2), finite-horizon costs (E1, E2, and the revenue decomposition in E5). It uses the theorems' own classes and couplings, so it is compatible with the theory by construction. It is cheap. Its scientific value exceeds a search-only study because certified intervals can be compared with the theorem's explicit finite-n constant L_n + 2 n s_n + 2 b_n(n) + R_K(n) (0.91 per round at n = 2,000, 0.41 at 20,000, 0.30 at 50,000; vacuous below n = 1,000), and because the current hand-picked deviations attain the L_n term exactly (claim matrix A2), so the paper can state that the rate of Theorem `thm:private-mesp` is attained by MESP as specified.

Narrative consequence that the main agent must weigh before approval. The certified lower bound is roughly three to ten times the gains now quoted in `appendix_experiments.tex` (0.091 per round certified at n = 2,000 versus 0.026 and 0.0094 quoted), and the sentence "primary shading eventually becomes costly" is true only of constant shading: boundary-tracking shading extracts about b_n(N_n) = Theta(sqrt(n log n)) at every horizon without triggering the monitor. This is consistent with the theorem (it is the 2 b_n(n) term) and makes the paper more honest, but Figure 1(a) and the surrounding text must be rewritten. A note for the theory lead (not an edit request from me): Scout A2 observes that the theorem's constant is dominated by 2 n s_n, a term the proof charges to the deviator but not to the baseline, so the explicit constant may be tightenable by accounting.

## 3. Core experiments

### E1. Exact and searched exploitability of MESP over B_{i,n} (evidence: exact computation on synthetic instances)

Question. How large is the largest unilateral gain over S_star at finite n on each instance, how does it decay, and how does it compare with the theorem's explicit bound and with the hand-picked deviations?

Formulation (Scout A2, Sections 2 and 3, which I re-derived independently in the claim matrix). Others at S_star are independent of the deviator, so the deviator faces single-agent stochastic control. Affinity: Q(s, m) = 1 + mu_i minus m + m W(s,1) + (1 minus m) W(s,0) is affine in m, so an optimal target is an endpoint (0 or M_i; mu_i or M_i if honest), randomization never helps, and the two-action recursion is exact over all of B_{i,n} for Stages 1 and 2. Stage 1: state (own pull r, own successes Z_r); the price on the winning event does not depend on the deviator's own bid, so its only Stage-1 lever is the shape of its bid distribution; the adaptive rule "deliver M_i until the count reaches z-star, then 0" makes the bid nearly deterministic and beats every fixed target (56 utility beyond the best fixed underbid at n = 2,000 in unique_top). Terminal value from the exact law of the others' maximum bid and tie count (products of binomial pmfs and cdfs, uniform priority as in `run.py`). Stage 2: state (r, Z_r) alive iff Z_r >= r tau minus b_n(r), two actions, dead states receive the fallback continuation F(N_n minus r minus 1); one sweep costs 0.9 s at n = 20,000 and about 4 s at 50,000 per price value; about 250 and 370 price values carry mass above 10^(-12), the rest charged to the upper bound. Loser branch: bounded by 2 N_n (n^(-4) + (2 n^3)^(-1)), below 10^(-6) for n >= 1,000. Fallback: F_LB = 0 (certified; per-pull utility is nonnegative), F_UB = min{2H, (1 + mu_i minus V_i) H + R_K(H)} for a top deviator from eq. private-fallback-top (valid under any information relaxation), min{2H, 2 R_K(H)/delta_i} for a non-top deviator (vacuous for delta_i = 0.005; only the lower bound is reportable there and the write-up says so). Slack of the sandwich at most max_H [R_K(H) minus s_n H] plus lower order: 40 utility at n = 2,000, 73 at 20,000, 94 at 50,000 (0.020, 0.0036, 0.0019 per round). Information structure: the lower bound is computed under the blind model I0 (own history plus announced tau), the upper bound under the relaxation I1 (all other bids and the priority known before Stage 1), which covers either reading of the sentence in `setting.tex` about the private mechanism revealing history. Certified quantities: Exploit_LB = V^{I0}_{F=0} minus u_i^{UB}(S_star) and Exploit_UB = V^{I1}_{F_UB} + loser term minus u_i^{LB}(S_star), both exact double-precision numbers with the baseline computed by the same forward recursion (rounding monitored with `numpy.longdouble` on three configurations).

Pilot numbers already in hand (Scout A2, login node, seconds; unique_top of `scalar_incentives/run.py`, K = 3, focal agent 0): n = 500: LB 62.3, UB 87.5 (0.125 and 0.175 per round); n = 1,000: 108.0, 151.1 (0.108, 0.151); n = 2,000: 181.9, 239.7 (0.091, 0.120), of which 125.9 is Stage-2 boundary tracking (b_n(N_n) = 126.1) and 56.0 adaptive Stage-1 underbidding; tied_top at n = 2,000: 69.4, 144.7 (0.035, 0.072); n = 20,000 Stage 2 alone at tau = V_i minus s_n gains 551.2 (b_n(N_n) = 551.6). Heuristic extrapolation of the lower bound per round: 0.044 at 20,000 and 0.032 at 50,000, to be replaced by the exact runs. Go criteria already met: LB below UB, both below the theorem constant, LB per round decreasing in n.

Instances and horizons. The unified parameter-named instance table of Scout A4 (Section 5.2 of its report) replaces the two inconsistent tables; the five current instances become named members (unique_top is K3, gap .25, low means, honest non-top, focal top; low_gap_nontop is the gap .005 point). Horizons {200, 500, 1000, 2000, 5000, 8000, 20000, 50000}; K = 2 instances at n up to 2,000 to answer the brief's K = 2 question; a gap grid {0, .01, .02, .05, .10, .20} at K = 3 and K = 2. At n at most 1,000 the price is degenerate (s_n = .59 at n = 200 makes tau = 0 for second capacities below .6), so small-horizon exploitability is dominated by Stage-1 effort savings and the figure says so.

Searched part (validation and fallback probe). CMA-ES over an 8-parameter bang-bang threshold class (Stage 1: deliver M_i iff Z_r < c_1 L_n + c_2 sqrt(L_n); Stage 2: deliver M_i iff the slack Z_r minus (r tau minus b_n(r)) is below c_3 + c_4 sqrt(N_n minus r)/sqrt(N_n) + c_5 (V_i minus tau); fallback: deliver M_i iff own recent selection share is below c_7 while more than c_8 H rounds remain), population 16, 150 generations, 64 common-random-number episodes per fitness evaluation, 4 restarts, selection on a 512-seed validation block, one evaluation on 4,000 held-out paired seeds; the lower confidence endpoint is the searched lower bound. A 16-unit GRU alternative exists if the parametric class falls short of Exploit_LB by more than 5 percent; CPU only unless that happens (GPU budget stays at zero planned hours). Reporting rule fixed now: "the best deviation found gains g per round (95 percent CI) at n on instance I; a lower bound on exploitability for B_{i,n} restricted to the searched class; it lies below the certified upper bound by d". Never "no profitable deviation exists".

Variants. The same recursion gives exploitability for every E2 variant with a price and a monitored winner (no monitor: trivially about tau N_n; permanent exclusion: continuation 0 after a switch, so exact without a bracket; UCB fallback: lower bound only, no appendix bound applies).

Supports: a certified interval containing the exact supremum gain over B_{i,n} on each tested instance and horizon (Stages 1 and 2 exact; fallback sandwiched), its decomposition, its decay in n against the explicit bound. Does not support: equilibrium as an empirical fact; anything about untested instances, coalitions, or vanishing gaps; optimality of searched fallback policies.

### E2. Mechanism baselines and ablation ladder (evidence: synthetic, paired simulation)

Mechanisms (Scout A3, Section 2): MESP; ESP-NoMon (second price, no monitor, no fallback); ESP-Mon-NoFB (monitor, round-robin fallback); ESP-Excl (noisy Pure-SP: monitor, permanent exclusion, promotion of the next bidder at (beta_(3) minus s_n)_+ with a fresh monitor; this is the faithful adaptation of Esmaeili, Shin, Slivkins Algorithm 2, which is the negative example in their own paper, verified at arXiv 2312.07929 and the AAAI record with DOI 10.1609/aaai.v39i13.33510); ESP-UCBfb (fresh symmetric UCB fallback); ESP-UCBfb-warm and warm-started EXP3 (supplement); symmetric UCB under all-maximum play (the public mechanism); the four existing non-mechanism references kept only as labeled reference lines (capacity UCB made symmetric, intrinsic UCB, uniform, oracle second price). Noisy SP+SAMF (their Algorithm 1 adapted) is a stretch item because its blocking margin has no principled setting under noise.

Ladder (Scout A3, Section 3): R1 bidding length L in {L_n, ceil(n^(2/3)), ceil(sqrt n), 1, floor(n/2K)} with s recomputed; R2 price in {(beta_(2) minus s_n)_+, beta_(2), confidence 1/n}; R3 monitor in {anytime b_n, none, no-slack, windowed}; R4 fallback in {fresh EXP3, round robin, unmonitored runner-up}; R5 fresh versus warm start; R6 EXP3 versus UCB versus exclusion; R7 symmetric versus index-order initialization (supplement); R8 ETC ignoring versus using exploitation rewards (supplement, contextual). A boundary-riding hand-picked deviation (shade by .05 for the first (1 minus eps) log(2 n^4)/(2 times .05^2) primary pulls, then return to tau) joins the menu so the search does not appear to discover what the theory already contains.

Behaviors. Type P, same behavior across mechanisms: S_star (all-max where no price exists), the extended hand-picked menu, and joint adversarial profiles for the robustness claim (all strategic agents bait-and-switch; sequential shading after each switch or exclusion; strategic agents deliver 0 in fallback; adaptive fallback behavior). Type M, each mechanism under its own best tested response: the E1 exact value where available, else the best hand-picked or searched deviation, labeled a lower bound.

Metrics (definitions in Scout A4, Section 2): revenue gap to M_(2) n per round; honest-benchmark gap under joint adversarial profiles; exploitability (exact or lower bound); false-switch frequency on a compliant winner (0 of R with the Clopper-Pearson upper bound for the anytime monitor, estimated for ablated monitors); median detection delay under bait-and-switch and shade .05 against log(2 n^4)/(2 epsilon^2) (which the committed data match within 4 percent wherever uncensored); fallback length; allocation shares; runtime only from the pinned single-process benchmark job.

Predicted readings, to be reported as measured either way: R3 and R4 are where MESP should dominate clearly (ESP-NoMon linearly exploitable; round-robin fallback falls below mu_H^star when the honest agent has the lowest capacity); R1, R2, R5, R6 are where an ablated variant may look better at these horizons (uncorrected price adds about s_n per round of revenue in unique-top instances while producing false switches with probability of order .25 in tied instances; UCB fallback beats EXP3 by the log n versus sqrt(n log n) gap under fixed post-switch targets; exclusion matches or beats MESP under single deviations and loses only under sequential joint deviations or false switches). The paper presents these as the price of the guarantees, with the theorem slack drawn as a reference line.

Supports: which component buys which guarantee at what finite-horizon price on the tested instances and behaviors. Does not support: optimality of any constant; coalition-proofness beyond the specific joint profiles; dominance of MESP on performance metrics.

### E3. Exact fixed-map exploitability and labeled stress tests (evidence: exact computation and synthetic simulation)

E3a Static Grid-ETC within S_HDS. Under fixed maps each cell's rewards are i.i.d. with the cell-average target (Lemma `lem:cell-law`), exploration ignores rewards, and commitment is a function of exploration counts, so the deviator's utility is a sum over cells of U_C(m_bar) = a_{i,C} [E min(V_C, K q_n)/K + E(V_C minus K q_n)_+ p_i(m_bar)], with a_{i,C} = 1 + mu_bar_{i,C} minus m_bar, V_C ~ Binomial(n, nu(C)), and p_i a degree-q_n Bernstein polynomial computed from the others' binomial laws with uniform priority. The supremum over full-profile maps is the sum of per-cell one-dimensional maxima; over own-context maps it separates by slab; a grid of step 10^(-6) has certified error below n (1 + q_n) delta, about one utility unit at n = 32,000. Horizons {2,000, 8,000, 32,000, 128,000, 512,000, 2 x 10^6}, seconds to minutes each; the exploration share (nominal 100, 94, 65, 61, 34, 30 percent; realized 91, 76, 63 percent in the current data at the first three) is plotted alongside because it explains why in-class gains decay only like n^(-1/6). Instance: Scout A2's theorem-compliant environment (K = 3, d = 1: M_1 = M_2 = .75 constant; M_3(x_3) = .75 minus .25 (0.6 minus x_3)_+; mu_i = .16 + .04 x_i for agents 1 and 2, .20 + .04 x_3 for agent 3; agent 2 honest), whose separator B = {x_3 = 0.6} is closed, null, and real (top set {1,2} below, {1,2,3} above), exercising both proof branches; the current instance is kept as a second configuration only if space allows, and the appendix sentence about the separator is corrected either way. Cross-checks: the exact formula at the five current maps must reproduce `contextual_incentives/summary.csv` within its intervals; a CMA-ES search over 32-bin local maps must not exceed the exact slab optimum; a batched cell-wise evaluator must reproduce `run.py`'s sequential path on shared uniforms.

E3b Out-of-scope stress tests, labeled "outside S_HDS; the theorem makes no claim and its proof is known to fail for them". (a) Count-based explore-then-shade, feasible under the blind model (deliver M_i on the first q_n own selections in a cell, then mu_i or 0): exact by the same decomposition, linear gain of order 0.1 per round at n = 32,000 for a top deviator; (b) reward-adaptive shading, simulated with 240 paired replicates; the same tests on Grid-UCB, which keeps updating, as the informative comparison. Separate hatched panel, never mixed with E3a.

E3c Public UCB within S_HI. Grid of 41 fixed targets for the focal agent against all-maximum play with common reward tapes, tied-top versus unique-top, n in {2,000, 8,000, 20,000, 50,000}; the maximum paired gain per round is the grid-resolved in-class exploitability. Expected: tied-top gain per round decreasing toward zero; unique-top gain linear at about the slack per round. This is the only experiment that shows the two-top condition doing work, and it is the first experiment for Theorem `thm:public-ucb` (claim matrix C3).

E3d Structural checks for the supplement: pathwise monotonicity of Grid-ETC over 10^4 coupled paths (zero violations expected; one violation is a bug in code or theorem); FATA and symmetric-initialization check for UCB with two tied top agents (R7).

### E4. Non-synthetic evaluation: scikit-learn provider bank (evidence: real outcomes, scripted strategies)

Design (Scout A1, Section 3.1, adopted with two changes). Data: Fashion-MNIST (MIT, verified 17 September 2026 at github.com/zalandoresearch/fashion-mnist) and UCI Covertype (CC BY 4.0, verified at archive.ics.uci.edu/dataset/31/covertype), downloaded to the scratchpad only, with license texts recorded in `audit/round2/A/DATA_LICENSES.md` at download time; scikit-learn (BSD-3) installed into the venv. Providers: K = 3 or 4 frozen ensembles (random forests of 256 trees; one HistGradientBoosting model) trained on deliberately different subsets so heterogeneity comes from training data. Effort knob: the number of trees or boosting stages evaluated at inference (menu {1, 2, 4, ..., 256}), compute cost recorded; targets below the cheapest level by scripted sabotage with a stated probability (the operational meaning of negative effort). Operational definitions on a measurement half of the held-out pool: A_i(x, k) = P(correct | context cell x, knob k); mu_i(x) := A_i(x, k_min); M_i(x) := max_k A_i(x, k); curves reported unsmoothed. Contexts: provider-local cheap-pass confidence quantized to [0,1] (d = 1, D = K), computed platform-side from frozen models, so profiles are i.i.d. and exogenous. Outcomes: correctness against the stored true label on the play half; each round is a real provider answer to a real task drawn with replacement. Mechanisms: MESP and the E2 variants on the pooled (context-free) accuracies so the scalar theorem applies as stated; Grid-UCB, Grid-ETC, context-blind UCB, LinUCB on the quantized local contexts. Behaviors: S_star, all-max, the hand-picked menu translated into knob schedules, the joint adversarial profiles. Changes to the scout's design: the scalar and contextual tracks are separated as above, and the top structure is not tuned; whatever the providers have (unique or near-tied) is reported, with both datasets used so both regimes are likely.

Pilot and drop rule. Train providers, measure curves, check: A_i(x, k) nondecreasing in k up to noise (every violation reported); spread of M_i above .05; at least one dataset with a top gap above 2 s_n at n = 20,000 and one with a near tie; MESP's mean price within s_n of the measured second capacity. If it fails, redesign once; if it fails again by 21 September, adopt P2.

Wording fixed now: "Real labeled tasks and real provider answers; the effort choice is scripted." Never "deployment", never "real strategic agents". Supports: MESP's price tracks the second measured capacity and revenue approaches M_(2) on real outcome draws that are not Bernoulli by construction; the monitor detects scripted bait-and-switch; the honest benchmark holds under scripted joint profiles; grid rules track real cell-wise means never verified to be Lipschitz. Does not support: human or real strategic behavior, equilibrium, cost linearity (the real compute cost is reported next to the model's effort convention), generality beyond these providers, menus, and pools.

## 5. Rejected ideas and reasons

- Human-subject study: excluded by the user's decision; infeasible before 28 September; IRB.
- Paid LLM API providers: unconfirmed resource, non-reproducible over time; S1 gives real LLM outputs without inference.
- Local CPU LLM inference (Qwen family, Apache 2.0): hundreds of core-hours with engineering risk; S1 dominates.
- Chess-puzzle testbed (Lichess CC0, Stockfish GPLv3): license-clean and real-outcome but heterogeneity would be manufactured by weakening one engine and the domain does not match the motivation.
- RouterBench: no dataset license on the card; a vendor-tier knob reinterprets the model.
- Crowd-label corpora as evidence about effort: no public corpus with worker ids, gold labels, and an effort intervention was found (Scout A1 checked CIFAR-10H, CIFAR-10N, Toloka, TREC 2010, Snow et al., Bluebirds, the Zheng collection, and five human-subject studies); allowed only as calibration of intrinsic means (S5).
- Stack Exchange, oDesk, MIMIC-IV, Codeforces, Galaxy Zoo, HELM: no verifiable outcome, no counterfactual allocation, access restrictions, or no compute knob.
- Discretized-target dynamic programming with an approximation-error analysis: unnecessary because the one-step objective is affine; Scout A2 records the grid bound (3 n delta for the Stage-2 recursion, (T + T^2) delta in general) for any future non-affine variant.
- Exact relaxed DP over the full EXP3 weight vector: intractable (reachable set grows like (2K)^t) and unnecessary given the sandwich slack of at most 0.02 per round.
- Search-only or GPU-first exploitability: rejected as primary; kept as validation.
- Black-box search over contextual maps as the primary method: unnecessary because the in-class problem separates exactly cell by cell; kept as a cross-check.
- A large final suite before pilots: every component has a pilot with a go criterion (Section 8).
- Presenting exclusion-based variants as strictly worse than MESP: rejected in advance; they are expected to match or beat MESP under single deviations and the paper reports what is measured.
- Windowed or single-pull monitors as "the earlier rule": exclusion-on-shortfall is the negative example in Esmaeili et al.; it enters as ablation rungs R3 and R6 with that framing.
- Runtime and memory as headline metrics: supplementary table only.
- Reporting "0 false switches" as a probability estimate: the bound is (2 n^3)^(-1), unresolvable by simulation; reported as 0 of R with the Clopper-Pearson upper bound plus the monitor-only Monte Carlo as a diagnostic that the union bound is loose.

## 9. Statistical and reproducibility protocol (adopted from Scout A4)

- Coupling: per-trial `SeedSequence.spawn(5)` children (Stage-1 tape, primary tape, fallback tape, mechanism randomness, deviation randomness), outcomes 1{U < m}; one mechanism module.
- Intervals: Student t primary; BCa via `scipy.stats.bootstrap(paired=True)` with per-(cell, metric) child seeds secondary; Clopper-Pearson for proportions with the rule-of-three upper bound at zero counts; stratified reporting (p_flip, E[gain | flip], E[gain | no flip]) in winner-flip cells.
- Replication: 240 pairs per incentive cell; 200 per mechanism or ablation cell (paired SD of reward per round 0.007 to 0.013 at n at least 8,000, so 0.002 per round is detectable); 100 per contextual performance cell; 240 per contextual incentive cell; one 1,000-pair profile-only cell per instance at n = 8,000; monitor-only Monte Carlo 10^5 paths per (tau, n).
- Hyperparameters: theorem constants frozen; baseline constants chosen on phase-0 seeds only and recorded in `PROTOCOL.md`; searched best responses trained on phase 2, selected on a validation child, reported once on phase 3.
- Seeds: `SeedSequence([20260828, suite_id, phase_id, instance_index, n, rep])`, phase_id in {0 pilot, 1 final, 2 train, 3 eval, 9 analysis}; `--quick` replaced by `--phase pilot --reps 20`.
- Multiple comparisons: one pre-registered primary contrast family per figure (the quoted numbers) with Holm-adjusted p-values or Bonferroni simultaneous intervals; all other cells exploratory with pointwise bands; rate claims as log-log slopes with delta-method intervals.
- Schema, manifest, storage, commands, compute log: Scout A4 Sections 8 and 9 verbatim (long-format `trials.csv`, `manifest.json` with versions, commit, node, command, sha256; runs under `<scratchpad>/round2/A/runs/<phase>/<suite>/`; accepted finals copied to the repository by a logged step; `run.py --phase --master --reps --horizons --instances --workers --array-task --array-size --benchmark` with a dependent `merge.py`; `COMPUTE_LOG.md` rows with `sacct` TotalCPU).

## 10. Claims the package would and would not support

Would support (evidence category in parentheses; bracketed values filled by the final runs):
1. On each tested instance and horizon up to 50,000, the supremum unilateral gain over B_{i,n} against MESP lies in a certified interval [LB, UB] per round (Stages 1 and 2 exact, fallback sandwiched); it decays with a log-log slope in [1/2, 2/3], lies an order of magnitude below the theorem's explicit constant, exceeds the hand-picked deviations by a factor of about [3 to 10], and decomposes into adaptive underbidding of about (M_i minus V_i) L_n and boundary tracking of about b_n(N_n), so the L_n and b_n terms of the bound are attained up to constants (exact computation; synthetic instances).
2. Removing the monitor makes the mechanism linearly exploitable; removing the price correction adds about s_n per round of revenue in unique-top instances and produces false switches with probability of order .25 in tied instances; removing the fallback or making exclusion permanent loses the honest benchmark under sequential joint shading [if E2 acceptance (iv) holds]; fresh restart and EXP3 versus UCB fallback show [measured] differences under the tested behaviors (synthetic, paired).
3. Within S_HDS, Static Grid-ETC's exact exploitability per round on the two-component instance decays at the theorem's rate and is exploration-dominated at the paper's horizons; count- and phase-dependent play outside S_HDS gains linearly, as the theorem's scope statement anticipates (exact computation and synthetic simulation).
4. Within S_HI, symmetric UCB is exploitable by a unique top agent at a linear rate and not by a tied top agent beyond a vanishing per-round gain (grid-resolved; synthetic).
5. On real labeled tasks answered by real providers with a compute knob, MESP's price tracks the second measured capacity, its revenue approaches M_(2), the monitor detects scripted bait-and-switch, and the honest benchmark holds under scripted joint profiles (real outcomes, scripted strategies).

Would not support: equilibrium in any class as an empirical fact; exploitability outside the tested instances, classes, or horizons; uniformity over vanishing gaps; coalition-proofness beyond the specific joint profiles; optimality of searched fallback policies; claims about human or real strategic behavior; effort claims from crowd data; dominance of MESP over exclusion-based variants on performance metrics; optimality of any constant; runtime claims beyond the pinned single-process measurements; a positive finite-horizon gain as a counterexample to an o(n) statement, or a decaying gain as a proof of it.
